I asked Claude, Anthropic's AI, to help me answer one question: could the new "plane sound reveals your location" trick help find MH370? It can't. But it led to a full reanalysis of where the plane went, built only from data anyone can download, and an honest look at why a quarter of a million square kilometres of searching has come up empty.

This is an independent analysis, not an official one. It does not find MH370.

Code, data sources, full reports and an interactive map: https://github.com/jazizwork/mh_370

KEY FINDINGS
• Satellite data: median end point 38.2°S on the 7th arc (90%: 36.5–39.5°S), in line with the Australian defence scientists' analysis. On its own it can't separate 38°S from the 34.2°S hotspot searched in 2025–26. That route fits only if the plane was descending during the 02:40 call; flown level, the frequency during that call is off by 39 Hz.
• Debris drift: models built from ocean drifters move debris about 20% too slowly, which pushed several studies north. Corrected, the debris points to about 31–35°S. The flaperon's measured drift points to about 38°S.
• Sound: no underwater-microphone or seismic candidate holds up on open data. The "Java event" is a natural ~M4.1 earthquake.
• WSPR: a 777 can change a WSPR reading by about 0.03 dB. Readings come in 1 dB steps and naturally jump 3.5–5 dB. A public blind test would settle it.
• The empty searches are evidence: 86% of the satellite probability is on seabed already searched. Counting that, a deliberately vague "model is wrong somewhere" alternative rises from 1 in 10 to nearly 1 in 2.

WHERE THE PROBABILITY IS NOW (each about 7,500 km²)
1 · 30.46°S 98.41°E · 42–62 km beyond the arc, along the 2018 search edge · ~8.5%
2 · 38.75°S 89.02°E · 90–130 km beyond the arc, glide zone · ~5%
3 · 32.36°S 96.89°E · 62–82 km beyond the arc · ~4%
Satellite-only pick · 39.94°S 84.82°E · on the arc, just south of the 2014–17 search
No single area holds more than about 1 chance in 10, and small changes to the assumptions can swap areas 1 and 2. Area 1 is near the WSPR crash claim but not on it; this analysis doesn't use WSPR.

CAVEATS
Independent and not peer-reviewed. How much to doubt the satellite model is a judgement call. There is no public fuel-burn model; the 2025–26 search footprint is reconstructed from ship tracking; how thoroughly past searches covered their ground is partly estimated. Full caveats are in the repo.
