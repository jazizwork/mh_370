# WSPR / GDTAAA "passive radar" claims about MH370: claims, method, evidence, and catalogue of critiques (audit as of 5 Nov 2026)

Notes on sourcing: much primary WSPR material is self-published by proponents (mh370search.com, Dropbox PDFs) and amplified by AirlineRatings (Geoffrey Thomas, a consistent promoter). Much critique is on Victor Iannello's blog (mh370.radiantphysics.com) and in comment threads. Neither side has a peer-reviewed paper on this question that I could find. Labels used below: **[Primary-proponent]**, **[Primary-critic]**, **[Official]**, **[Secondary]**, **[My inference]** (my own reasoning or arithmetic, not from a source).

## Q1. Who is involved, what reports exist, and has anything been peer-reviewed or validated (incl. the University of Liverpool study)?

### Takeaway
The WSPR/GDTAAA case comes from Richard Godfrey, with Dr Hannes Coetzee (ZS6BZP) and Prof. Simon Maskell (Liverpool) as later co-authors. Dr Robert Westphal originated the idea in 2020. It is set out in a run of self-published reports from 2021 to 2025, and the claimed impact point has moved from 33.177°S 95.300°E (2021) to 29.128°S 99.934°E ±30 km (2023/24). As of Nov 2026 I found **no peer-reviewed publication and no published results from the Liverpool validation study**. In May 2026 Maskell was still describing it as an open question ("we're trying to figure out who's right").

### Inferences
- [My inference] Maskell's co-authorship of the 2023 and 2024 reports means the Liverpool study is not fully independent of the hypothesis it tests. Its value will depend on a pre-registered, blind protocol with control links, and none has been published.
- [My inference] Five years after the first claim (May 2021 to Nov 2026), there is no peer-reviewed validation, the "within six months" (2024) and "months" (May 2025) timelines have both passed, and the lead proponent's 2026 post no longer advances a WSPR crash location. On balance that is weak evidence that the large-scale study has not produced a clear positive result. It does not prove a negative result.

## Q2. How does the method work, step by step (fields, anomaly definition, link selection, geometry, time resolution)?

### Takeaway
The method is statistical post-processing of WSPRnet **spot metadata**: integer SNR, received frequency, and integer drift. For a Tx–Rx link, a value more than **0.75 SD** from that link's mean over **±3 hours** is called an "anomaly". It counts as a "detection" if the link's great circle (extended via Proplab ray tracing) passes through a candidate aircraft position in that 2-minute slot. The track is built forward under a speed constraint and alignment with waypoints, and is checked against Inmarsat BTO/BFO and drift results. The thresholds and definitions changed over time (0.5 SD → 0.75 SD; frequency anomalies added in late 2023).

### Cited Findings
**Data fields available (WSPRnet / wspr.live schema)**
- wspr.live `rx` table: snr Int8 "Reported SNR in dB" (integer dB); drift Int8 "-3 to 3" (wspr.live says the unit is unknown); frequency in Hz; distance, azimuth and rx_azimuth as integers; tx/rx lat/lon "represent the center of the reported locator box". Data are raw and "might be duplicates, false spots and other errors." — [wspr.live](https://wspr.live/)
- WSPR protocol: 110.6 s transmission, about 6 Hz bandwidth, 4-FSK at 1.4648 baud, transmissions starting 1 s into even minutes, 50-bit message, normally a 4-character Maidenhead locator. — [Wikipedia WSPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WSPR_(Amateur_radio_software))

**Anomaly definition (proponents' own words, Feb 2024 paper)**
- "WSPRnet reports the Signal to Noise Ratio (SNR) referenced to a 2,500 Hz bandwidth… typical values are -30 dB to +20 dB… A temporary change in the SNR or received frequency by more than 0.75 of a standard deviation (SD) measured over a ±3 hour time period can be caused by an aircraft crossing the propagation path. The measured drift… typical values are -3 Hz/minute to +3 Hz/minute… When there is no systematic drift, a temporary drift can be caused by an aircraft crossing the propagation path." — [Godfrey/Coetzee/Maskell PDF, 15 Feb 2024, p.1](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/vzftcvfx01lhbt3xfgyu5/How-Does-WSPR-Detect-Aircraft-over-Long-Distances-15FEB2024.pdf?rlkey=p8dcu8q3ww741joa922bdikng&dl=0)
- Worked example from the paper: link mean SNR −25.0 dB, SD 2.0 dB; actual −22 dB counts as an anomaly (a 3 dB, i.e. 3-quantum, change). Drift anomalies cited are "non systematic drift anomaly of -1 Hz/minute". — same PDF, pp.15, 46, 54
- Threshold history: the second analysis "tightened the definition of an anomaly from ≥0.5 SD to ≥0.75 SD", which cut anomalous positions from 113 to 77. Changing the "accuracy of the transmitter and receiver antenna locations" raised this to 125 anomalies at 65 positions. Adding frequency anomalies raised it to 313 at 130 positions. — [mh370search.com 22 Dec 2023](https://www.mh370search.com/2023/12/22/mh370-search-area/)
- Godfrey's stated hypotheses (2023 comment). Null: "WSPRnet links show statistically significant anomalies when an aircraft is not on the great circle path". The alternative has "is on" in place of "is not on". — [mh370search.com Technical Report comments](https://www.mh370search.com/2022/03/14/mh370-wspr-technical-report/)

**Link selection and geometry**
- Core criterion (2022): "The alignment of the predicted position of MH370 with the great circle path of one or more anomalous WSPR links." — [mh370search.com 14 Mar 2022](https://www.mh370search.com/2022/03/14/mh370-wspr-technical-report/)
- Proplab Pro V3.1/3.2 (IRI-2007) ray tracing is used to confirm "Interim Landing Points" lie near the great circle. A typical long-range link is "5 hops", about 15,000 km. — [mh370search.com 22 Dec 2023](https://www.mh370search.com/2023/12/22/mh370-search-area/); [Feb 2024 PDF pp.2–3](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/vzftcvfx01lhbt3xfgyu5/How-Does-WSPR-Detect-Aircraft-over-Long-Distances-15FEB2024.pdf?rlkey=p8dcu8q3ww741joa922bdikng&dl=0)
- Track building: a maximum ground speed of 536.2 kt (33.1 km per 2 min); GDAS winds; up to four branch points for alternative paths, each dropped after 10 min without detections; each turn aligned with an aeronautical or geographic waypoint. "Clipping" distances of 2, 5 and 10 km from the aircraft were tested. Fresnel zones are not discussed. — [mh370search.com 22 Dec 2023](https://www.mh370search.com/2023/12/22/mh370-search-area/)
- Reflection model (THY161 test): Tx → aircraft → Rx distances of 16,699–36,300 km (mean 27,762 km). "We find the reflection is always 180.0° ±0.323°". For example, Tx IK5ZAI (Italy) → aircraft near Madagascar (about 7,900 km) → reflected "back" to Rx M0VSE (UK). — [Feb 2024 PDF pp.6, 10](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/vzftcvfx01lhbt3xfgyu5/How-Does-WSPR-Detect-Aircraft-over-Long-Distances-15FEB2024.pdf?rlkey=p8dcu8q3ww741joa922bdikng&dl=0)
- Locator precision: the paper concedes a 4-character locator is "highly inaccurate with a precision no better than ±120 km" and a 6-character one "±5 km". It says this was "solved with an accurate antenna database sourced from each antenna owner" to 6 decimal places. That database is not public. — same PDF p.2
- RCS: the paper cites DSTG modelling of "up to 45 dB m2" for a B777-size aircraft and argues "Calculating the RCS of an aircraft at HF is going to add little value, as WSPR is an encoded communications link and not a detection system such as radar." — same PDF p.2
- Coherent integration: "The WSPR transmission time is 110.6 seconds and exceeds the Coherent Integration Time (CIT) of the ionosphere, which is typically no more than 60 seconds." Yet the conclusion credits "The long coherent integration time of the WSPRnet receivers" for detection. — same PDF pp.3, 61
- Time resolution: 2-minute WSPR slots. Godfrey (2022): it "provided the findings every two minutes during the entire flight." — [mh370search.com 2022](https://www.mh370search.com/2022/03/14/mh370-wspr-technical-report/)
- Role of Inmarsat and drift data: Godfrey says they "do not serve as prior knowledge but solely as confirmation." — [mh370search.com 22 Dec 2023](https://www.mh370search.com/2023/12/22/mh370-search-area/)
- Other aircraft: Godfrey says the team checks "the ADS-B data of all other aircraft" against WSPR paths. — [mh370search.com 15 Feb 2024](https://www.mh370search.com/2024/02/15/wspr-aircraft-tracking/). For 2014 MH370-era data, global ADS-B coverage over the Indian Ocean was sparse.

### Inferences
- [My inference] The "180.0° ±0.323°" finding follows from the selection rule rather than confirming it. Links are chosen because their great circle passes through the aircraft position, so the bearings are collinear by construction. It is not independent evidence of reflection.
- [My inference] The 180° geometry means the receiver sits on the transmitter side, e.g. Italy→UK, a direct short path of roughly 1,300–1,600 km. The decoded SNR, frequency and drift would then be dominated by that direct (or normal skywave) path, which is exactly Iannello's masking argument (Q4).
- [My inference] Several free parameters were tuned with knowledge of the expected answer: the SD threshold (0.5→0.75), the ±3 h window, which fields count (frequency added in 2023), clipping radius, branch-point rules, waypoint snapping, and the antenna-location corrections. This is a garden of forking paths.
- [My inference] A ±3 h baseline spans large diurnal changes (terminator, MUF changes). A departure from a 6-hour mean is not a departure from the local trend, so the definition will flag many ordinary propagation changes.
- [My inference] Minor quality issues in the Feb 2024 PDF: the sign convention for deviations is inverted (actual −22 vs mean −14 is called "+8.0 dB"), and a 14:46 table references "the 14:44 UTC WSPR time slot". These do not change conclusions but point to limited internal review.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain the exact GDTAAA scoring code. It is not public, which is itself a reproducibility problem.
- I found no proponent document that treats Fresnel-zone width or multi-hop path ambiguity quantitatively.
- I could not obtain the definitions of "duplicate" links used in the 2022 ROC analysis.

## Q3. What evidence supports the WSPR case? (Steelman)

### Takeaway
The strongest honest case is this. HF skywave detection of aircraft is physically real; JORN-style OTH radar exists. WSPRnet is a huge multi-static, multi-frequency dataset. Proponents report above-chance ROC curves (AUC about 0.58–0.67) and post-hoc agreement between anomalous links and known aircraft positions (THY161, the short-range flights, 48 B777 flights), plus consistency with the Inmarsat/drift-constrained region. A professor of statistics is engaged, and one independent student study found "preliminary" support while declining to confirm it. None of this is a blind, pre-registered test.

### Cited Findings
- OTHR precedent cited by proponents: NRL 1956 experiments showed "the ionosphere is generally sufficiently stable for an HF sky wave radar to succeed for over-the-horizon aircraft detection"; DSTG RCS modelling gives up to 45 dBsm. — [Feb 2024 PDF pp.2–3, 60](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/vzftcvfx01lhbt3xfgyu5/How-Does-WSPR-Detect-Aircraft-over-Long-Distances-15FEB2024.pdf?rlkey=p8dcu8q3ww741joa922bdikng&dl=0)
- Scale: about 6 million distinct links over 3,000 km in 2023, 1.7 billion spots in 2023, about 7,500 links per 2 minutes. — [Feb 2024 PDF](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/vzftcvfx01lhbt3xfgyu5/How-Does-WSPR-Detect-Aircraft-over-Long-Distances-15FEB2024.pdf?rlkey=p8dcu8q3ww741joa922bdikng&dl=0); [mh370search.com Jan 2025](https://www.mh370search.com/2025/01/01/new-technology/)
- THY161 (11 Nov 2023, Antananarivo→Mauritius, A330-300): "We analysed 194 WSPR signals passing within the vicinity of flight THY161. There were 44 WSPR anomalies comprising 19 SNR anomalies, 21 frequency anomalies and 4 drift anomalies… at 13 out of 14 different points in time." Positions were compared with FlightAware ADS-B. — [Feb 2024 PDF p.9](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/vzftcvfx01lhbt3xfgyu5/How-Does-WSPR-Detect-Aircraft-over-Long-Distances-15FEB2024.pdf?rlkey=p8dcu8q3ww741joa922bdikng&dl=0)
- ROC results: cruise-phase AUC 58.66%, rising to 60.43% after reprocessing (70 observations), which Godfrey calls "Fair" not "Fail". A later AUC of about 67% (171 observations), which Godfrey says is "distant from y=x" while conceding WSPR is "a noisy sensor". — [AirlineRatings, 2 Nov 2022](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/critics-of-wsprnet-tracking-mh370-been-answered); [mh370search.com 22 Dec 2023](https://www.mh370search.com/2023/12/22/mh370-search-area/)
- Godfrey: "it is hard to argue that a Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve that is distant from the y=x line is measuring pure noise… It works every time, with different aircraft and in different locations and in different years." Five case studies (e.g., EK421, QTR901) were tested "against known radar, ADS-B or GPS data". — [mh370search.com 2022 thread, Sept 2023 comments](https://www.mh370search.com/2022/03/14/mh370-wspr-technical-report/)
- Claimed corroboration: the WSPR path "align[s] with the previous analyses by Boeing, Inmarsat and the drift analysis" and overlaps the UWA drift area. Godfrey also claims alignment with the captain's home-simulator path ("no coincidence", 17 Apr 2024). — [AirlineRatings](https://airlineratings.com/articles/two-new-reports-verify-mh370-location-technology); [mh370search.com home](https://www.mh370search.com/)
- Short-range physics is real. Nils Schiffhauer (a critic) measured aircraft Doppler scatter on a 250 kW CRI carrier at about 2.2 km slant range (scatter −105.9 dBm against noise −108 dBm), and Iannello reproduced it within 4 dB. This shows aircraft HF scatter exists but only at short range and high power. — [Iannello, 19 Dec 2021](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/2021/12/19/wspr-cant-find-mh370/)
- Independent student study (Singapore DSTA Young Defence Scientists Programme report, 19 Dec 2024, "Forward Scatter Aircraft Detection with Amateur Radio Network WSPRnet"), as quoted by Godfrey: "Our preliminary verification suggests that using standard score to find anomalies in SNR could be used to detect an aircraft flying through a WSPR link" but "We are unable to conclusively verify that data from WSPRnet can be used to detect and localise aircraft." — [mh370search.com comment, 9 May 2025](https://www.mh370search.com/2024/05/05/new-search/comment-page-10/). The DSTA PDF itself returned 403. It is listed as project ES011 at the Singapore Science & Engineering Fair 2025 — [SSEF 2025 list](https://file.go.gov.sg/ssefpreliminaryjudgingresults.pdf)
- Ed Anderson (370Location) is reported as saying the flaw in one critical paper (the "Azimuth Error" paper) was a misreading of WSPR data "not the WSPR approach itself". The source is AirlineRatings, a pro-Godfrey outlet. — [AirlineRatings](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/mh370-tracking-expert-responds-to-a-recent-paper). Note that Anderson elsewhere reported negative Doppler-vs-distance results (Q4).
- ATSB (Feb 2022) reportedly called Godfrey "a credible expert on the subject of MH370" but did not assess WSPR (see Q5). — [Jeff Wise](https://www.deepdivemh370.com/p/the-real-richard-godfrey-s2ep34-video)

### Inferences
- [My inference] Even taking the ROC numbers at face value, AUC of about 0.58–0.67 means a weak per-link discriminator. Fusing many weak, correlated detections across links that share transmitters and receivers can easily over-state certainty unless correlation is modelled. Proponents have not published such modelling.
- [My inference] Agreement with Inmarsat/drift constraints is not independent corroboration if the path search is steered by speed limits, waypoints and arc-consistent branch choices. Mike Glynn made this point (Q4).

### Gaps
- No blinded ROC analysis with a pre-specified threshold and published control-link sets has been made available for independent re-computation.

## Q4. Catalogue of substantive critiques and possible methodological mistakes, with Godfrey's replies

### Takeaway
The critiques fall into seven families: (A) physics and link budget, (B) data and protocol limits, (C) statistics, (D) path-first confirmation bias, (E) failed or non-blind validation tests, (F) geometry, and (G) field-specific artefacts. Points that are **checkable facts** are separated from **opinion** below. The strongest and best-documented are A, C, D and E. The frequency- and drift-field artefact points (G) are partly my own inference and should be treated as hypotheses to test.

### Cited Findings

**A. Physics / link budget (quantitative critiques; the assumptions are explicit and checkable)**
- Iannello, "WSPR Can't Find MH370" (19 Dec 2021), case HB9CZF→VK1CH (16,527 km, 14.097 MHz, 1 W): forward-scatter RCS about 18,791 m² (about 23× projected area); scattered signal about −210 dBm against direct about −110 dBm; noise reference −129 dBm. That gives S/N ≈ **−81 dB vs about −30 dB needed**: "5 orders of magnitude too weak to be detected and decoded by WSPR!" The method was calibrated against DK8OK's CRI measurement to within 4 dB. — [Iannello](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/2021/12/19/wspr-cant-find-mh370/)
- Masking: even if detectable, the scatter is about 100 dB below the direct path and "absolutely indistinguishable from the direct signal". WSPRnet records only the combined decoded signal, with no Doppler-separated component. — [Iannello](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/2021/12/19/wspr-cant-find-mh370/); restated on [Metabunk, Mendel, 5 Sep 2023](https://www.metabunk.org/threads/possible-location-of-missing-flight-mh370-found.13153/)
- Steve Kent (IG member, Johns Hopkins; posts as "sk999") wrote a paper that "formally treats skywave propagation and scatter off airplanes, and concludes there is insufficient signal strength (by many orders of magnitude)". He also made a 2025 video, which Iannello (X, Mar 2025) called "excellent… explaining why WSPR tracking of MH370 is complete rubbish. He supplies the scientific rigor that is absent in the WSPR proponents' papers." — [Iannello blog](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/2021/12/19/wspr-cant-find-mh370/); [Iannello on X](https://x.com/RadiantPhysics/status/1904681962188439706); Jeff Wise also points readers to "sk999's" video — [Wise, 29 Mar 2025](https://www.deepdivemh370.com/p/the-real-richard-godfrey-s2ep34-video). I did not locate the video URL itself.
- Nils Schiffhauer (DK8OK, 6 Dec 2021), from about 30 Doppler tracks: aircraft scatter changes the overall signal by "almost always well below 0.3 dB", while shortwave levels swing up to 30 dB within seconds, "the rule rather than the exception". WSPR does not record the ionospheric state that OTH radars measure. On Iannello's blog he wrote: "Charlatanry or pseudo-science is the right commentary." — [RTL-SDR summary](https://www.rtl-sdr.com/nils-critiques-the-mh370-wspr-aircraft-scatter-theory/); [Iannello comments](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/2021/12/19/wspr-cant-find-mh370/)

### Inferences
- [My inference, verified arithmetic] For a Gaussian variable, P(|z|>0.75) = 45.3%, matching Iannello's 45% chance rate. If SNR and frequency were independent and each tested at 0.75 SD, P(at least one "anomaly" per link) ≈ 1−(0.547)² ≈ 70%. With drift and several links per 2-minute slot, finding at least one anomaly near almost any hypothesised position in almost every slot is expected under the null. The proponents' figure of 130/189–195 slots "with anomalies" is therefore not evidence by itself.
- [My inference] In THY161, 44 field-anomalies (about 33 distinct anomalous links once the dual/triple overlaps are removed) out of 194 near-aircraft links is about 17–23%. That is *below* a naive Gaussian chance rate. No control set of links away from the aircraft in the same window is reported, so the test cannot show an excess anomaly rate. This is the single most damaging internal check, though non-Gaussian, integer-quantised SNR could change the exact null rate. Anyone could run this test on wspr.live.
- [My inference, hypothesis to test] **Frequency "anomalies" may be largely transmitter frequency-randomisation and receiver-calibration artefacts.** A uniform ±80–100 Hz random offset gives SD ≈ 46–58 Hz, comparable to the 56–79 Hz link SDs in the paper. Aircraft Doppler at these HF frequencies is at most about 2v/λ ≈ 13 Hz at 10 MHz and about 18 Hz at 14 MHz for 188 m/s, and the decoder reports the frequency of the dominant (direct) signal anyway. Adding frequency anomalies more than doubled the "detections" (125→313) without changing the path. That pattern is what one expects if the new anomalies are noise that can be made consistent with a pre-chosen path. I found **no published critic making this specific point**, so it is flagged as novel.
- [My inference] SNR quantisation: with link SDs of about 2–5 dB, a 0.75 SD threshold is crossed by 2–4 integer-dB steps, well within ordinary QSB fading (Schiffhauer's up to 30 dB in seconds). Drift is an integer −3..+3, so ±1 is the smallest non-zero quantum and routine (oscillator warm-up or thermal drift, decoder estimation). Treating it as an aircraft signature has no demonstrated basis.
- [My inference] Duplicate and correlated links: many links share a transmitter or receiver. A single ionospheric disturbance near a station would create "multiple intersecting anomalous links" that look like corroboration but are one event. This compounds the look-elsewhere problem.
- [My inference] Summary split. **Verified, checkable errors or contradictions**: the CIT contradiction; the 180° tautology; no control group in THY161; the threshold changed after the fact; failed or ambiguous validation tests as reported by a participant validator; the 17:16 spot is unremarkable statistically. **Strongly supported by physics but model-dependent**: the link-budget shortfall of about 50 dB and masking. **Opinion**: "pseudo-science", "junk science", credibility attacks.

## Q5. Did the 2025–26 Ocean Infinity search cover the WSPR point, and what have ATSB/Malaysia said?

### Takeaway
The 2025–26 Ocean Infinity search (no-find-no-fee, $70M, about 15,000 km² target) surveyed about **7,571 km²** over 28 days and found nothing (Malaysian MOT/AAIB, 8 Mar 2026). **No official source says whether the 29.128°S 99.934°E WSPR circle (about 2,830 km²) was searched.** Contemporaneous vessel tracking shows work near about 35°S 93°E (UGIB/CAPTION zones), and I found no evidence the vessel went to about 29°S 100°E. ## Q6. Have there been independent replications using public WSPR data (e.g., wspr.live)?

### Takeaway
There is **no independent, blinded, published replication** that confirms long-range aircraft detection from WSPRnet metadata. The closest independent work is a Singapore DSTA student study (Dec 2024): "unable to conclusively verify", with only "preliminary" SNR-anomaly support. Critics' re-analyses of specific MH370 spots found them unremarkable. A reader claim that "no independent researcher has been able to fully replicate Godfrey's WSPR path from scratch" is plausible but unsourced. Public data (wspr.live, ClickHouse SQL) plus ADS-B archives make a proper control-group test feasible.

### Cited Findings
- DSTA (Singapore) YDSP report, 19 Dec 2024, "Forward Scatter Aircraft Detection with Amateur Radio Network WSPRnet", as quoted by Godfrey: "We are unable to conclusively verify that data from WSPRnet can be used to detect and localise aircraft"; "Our preliminary verification suggests that using standard score to find anomalies in SNR could be used to detect an aircraft…"; "more research needs to be done". ### Inferences
- [My inference] A decisive, cheap test design: (1) pre-register thresholds, fields and window; (2) take N known flights (ADS-B) and, for each 2-minute slot, compare the anomaly rate on links whose great circle passes within X km of the aircraft against matched control links (same Tx/Rx stations, band and time of day) not near any ADS-B aircraft; (3) evaluate blind on hidden flight IDs; (4) correct for shared-station correlation and multiple comparisons; (5) repeat with frequency anomalies excluded and with transmitters known to randomise frequency excluded. Nobody has published this.
- [My inference] For 2014 Indian Ocean data, the near-absence of ADS-B coverage means other-aircraft interference cannot be excluded. Only a modern-data validation can establish the method before applying it to MH370.
