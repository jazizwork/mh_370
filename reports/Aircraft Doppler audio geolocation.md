# Passing propeller planes turn soundtracks into coordinates

The technique comes from a YouTube video by the OSINT creator **colsto**, "I discovered a sound that leaks your exact location (and it's everywhere)", uploaded on about **2 November 2026** ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UytYrhYLOKI); [Digg summary](https://digg.com/ai/7wr4rmyp)). The idea is that a passing propeller aircraft leaves a faint, often inaudible tone in a recording. As the plane passes, Doppler shift bends that tone into an S-shaped curve. The shape of the curve encodes the aircraft's speed, its closest-approach distance and the moment of closest approach. A brute-force search then asks where on the ground a microphone would have heard exactly those curves from aircraft recorded in public ADS-B flight-tracking archives. colsto says he placed a security-camera clip, known only to be from somewhere in the continental US on 8 September 2026, to within about **100 m** using **two flyovers 920 s apart** ([lilithai.dev](https://lilithai.dev/radar/i-discovered-a-sound-that-leaks-your-exact-location-and-it-s-everywhere?lang=en); [IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/inaudible-aircraft-sounds-reveal-recording-location-1829800)). He released no code. Within 72 hours, at least ten open-source reimplementations appeared, several written with AI coding agents. On favourable US recordings they report errors from about **30 m to a few hundred metres**, and they fail on many other recordings. The physics is not new: Australia's DSTO estimated aircraft speed and range from a single microphone's Doppler track in **1992**. What is new is running that physics in reverse against crowd-sourced flight archives, at continental scale. In practice the method needs a known date, at least two clean propeller flyovers, dense ADS-B coverage and an unedited audio track. When those conditions hold, it is a real privacy leak that stripping metadata does not close. Nothing about it is peer-reviewed, and it is only days old.

## A closed-source demo spawned a swarm of clones in 72 hours

colsto's demonstration used a nearly static security-camera recording of trees and a river. The recording was dated 8 September 2026, believed to be in the continental US, with the hour unknown ([pasqualepillitteri.it](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/30208/aircraft-noise-geolocates-video-doppler-effect)). He pulled two faint propeller-aircraft Doppler curves out of the spectrogram, 920 s apart. He then gridded the whole continental US at **200 m**, modelled when and how each tracked aircraft would have sounded at every grid point, discarded points where other aircraft should have been heard in the silence between events, and scored the surviving candidates against the spectrogram ([Digg](https://digg.com/ai/7wr4rmyp)). IBTimes reports that about 70,000 candidate points were narrowed to about 15,000 before one score stood out. A Street View check reportedly showed the tree line and a pole-mounted camera on a riverbank ([IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/inaudible-aircraft-sounds-reveal-recording-location-1829800)). Reimplementers identify the site as **Oyster Bay, Long Island, NY** ([doppler-locate](https://github.com/digitalis-p/doppler-locate); [dopplertrack](https://github.com/mjshiggins/dopplertrack)).

The video description reportedly cites months of work, more than 5,000 lines of code and about 50 GB of data, and says no tools would be released ([lilithai.dev](https://lilithai.dev/radar/i-discovered-a-sound-that-leaks-your-exact-location-and-it-s-everywhere?lang=en)). Sources disagree on which flight data colsto used. One reimplementer says the video used Flightradar24 and a 30 m elevation model ([doppler-locate](https://github.com/digitalis-p/doppler-locate)). A blog says it names OpenSky and adsb.lol ([pasqualepillitteri.it](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/30208/aircraft-noise-geolocates-video-doppler-effect)). colsto's real identity and the full video description were not established in this research.

The reimplementations followed quickly. **dopplertrack** by mjshiggins is the most-starred (147 stars at snapshot) and the one linked from Hacker News ([GitHub](https://github.com/mjshiggins/dopplertrack)). **doppler-locate** by digitalis-p is a Python, CUDA and GTK4 app with the most detailed README on failure modes ([GitHub](https://github.com/digitalis-p/doppler-locate)). **dopplerprint** was released on PyPI on 3 November ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/dopplerprint/)). **DOPPLERGEIST** was covered by VGTimes the same day ([VGTimes](https://vgtimes.com/tech-and-hardware/173330-dopplergeist-uses-aircraft-sounds-to-estimate-where-a-recording-was-made.html)). Others include **franken_doppler_finder**, a Rust port ([GitHub](https://github.com/Dicklesworthstone/franken_doppler_finder)); **doppler-geo**, which runs in the browser via WASM ([GitHub](https://github.com/teamchong/doppler-geo)); **flyover-locator** ([GitHub](https://github.com/amintt2/flyover-locator)); the **hermes** plugin ([GitHub](https://github.com/mojomast/hermes-aircraft-doppler-geolocation)); and **DopplerGPS**, framed as an academic artifact ([GitHub](https://github.com/dyneresearch/DopplerGPS)). The most rigorous physics write-up is **cbonitz/doppler-experiments**. A coding agent built it in about 27 minutes for about $10 and tested it against synthetic "worlds" ([GitHub](https://github.com/cbonitz/doppler-experiments); [pasqualepillitteri.it](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/30208/aircraft-noise-geolocates-video-doppler-effect)). How fast and cheaply these agent-built clones appeared became part of the story, and it is the strongest evidence that the core idea is reproducible.

Reaction was fast. A Lobsters submission scored **108 points** ([Lobsters](https://lobste.rs/s/s1btqo/this_background_sound_leaks_your_exact)), and Simon Willison called it a "genius piece of OSINT" ([simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/3/a-sound-that-leaks-your-exact-location/)). TechRadar ran the headline claim of 100 m from 60 seconds of audio ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/computing/cyber-security/youtuber-discovers-aircraft-noise-trick-that-can-geolocate-your-videos-to-within-100m-doppler-effect-pinpoints-locations-from-just-60-seconds-of-audio)). Skeptics questioned practicality rather than physics. On Hacker News, one commenter called two audible propeller passes "a pretty rare event", and another argued that extracting the curves still needs human judgment ([HN via Algolia](https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/items/50344762)). As of 5 November, no Bellingcat, GIJN or OSINT-newsletter coverage, peer-reviewed paper or preprint was found.

## One microphone and one S-curve yield speed, range and timing

The underlying physics is textbook kinematics. Take an aircraft in straight, level flight at speed *v*, emitting a steady tone *f₀*: for a propeller, the blade-passage frequency of roughly **50–150 Hz** and its harmonics ([cbonitz](https://github.com/cbonitz/doppler-experiments)). A stationary microphone hears `f = f₀ / (1 − M cos θ)`, where *M = v/c* and θ is the angle between the aircraft's velocity and the line to the listener at the moment of emission. On approach the received tone tends toward `f₊ = f₀/(1 − M)`. Receding, it tends toward `f₋ = f₀/(1 + M)`. Inverting gives the speed directly, `v = c(f₊ − f₋)/(f₊ + f₋)`, independent of the unknown *f₀*. At closest approach (CPA) the range rate is zero, so the received frequency equals *f₀* exactly. The slope there sets the slant range: `R_c = f₀v² / (c·|df/dt|)`. The centre of the S marks the CPA time on the recording's clock. In short, the frequency drop gives speed, the steepness gives distance, and the midpoint gives timing ([cbonitz](https://github.com/cbonitz/doppler-experiments)).

A worked example shows the scale. For *f₀* = 100 Hz and *v* = 70 m/s, the tone falls from about **125.6 Hz to 83.1 Hz**. A pass at 500 m slant range sweeps at about **−2.9 Hz/s** over roughly 7 seconds. At 3 km the slope flattens to about −0.48 Hz/s, a gentle curve that is easy to lose in noise. These figures are derived from the cited model, not taken from a source.

A subtlety is **retarded time**. Sound heard at time *t* left the aircraft at an earlier time τ, with `t = τ + R(τ)/c`. At 1 km that is about 2.9 s, during which a 70 m/s aircraft moves about 200 m. Reid, Zoubir and Boashash wrote this time–position relation explicitly into their 1997 flight-parameter model, along with a wind term ([CORE PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/15120179.pdf)). colsto's video reportedly warned that ignoring it biases the geometry. The cbonitz replication found the shape error from using reception time was up to 1.6 Hz but fell to near noise level after a time shift, and called the warning "overstated" ([cbonitz](https://github.com/cbonitz/doppler-experiments)).

The physics also explains why the method is restricted to **propeller aircraft**. Propeller and helicopter rotors produce stable narrowband harmonic lines whose source frequency stays constant in cruise. That constancy matters, because any rpm change during a pass is indistinguishable from Doppler shift. Jet noise is mostly broadband turbulence with no single frequency to track. Turbofan "buzz-saw" tones exist but are high-frequency, directional and tied to thrust setting, peaking near takeoff ([Han et al., Iowa State](https://www.aere.iastate.edu/caal/assets/papers/han_2014_mpt.pdf)). In doppler-locate's tests, including jets raised memory use to 34 GB, and the best jet-involving pair ranked only eighth ([doppler-locate](https://github.com/digitalis-p/doppler-locate)). Harmonics help in degraded audio. In cbonitz's synthetic telephone-band (300–3400 Hz) tests, the curve survived through upper harmonics, with **96% of frames recovered** when harmonics were strong and **51%** when they were weak ([cbonitz](https://github.com/cbonitz/doppler-experiments)).

A second, unexploited cue exists for jets. A near-ground microphone hears both the direct sound and a ground reflection delayed by about `2·h_mic·sin(elevation)/c`. The resulting comb-filter notches, known as **Lloyd's mirror** fringes, sweep through the spectrogram during a pass. Lo, Perry and Ferguson used them in 2002 to estimate jet speed and altitude from 10 jet transits ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/3003442_Aircraft_flight_parameter_estimation_using_acoustical_Lloyd's_mirror_effect)). The hermes clone has an experimental cepstrum-tracking mode based on the same principle ([hermes](https://github.com/mojomast/hermes-aircraft-doppler-geolocation)). For a phone held at 1.5 m, the first notch falls near 57 Hz with about 115 Hz spacing. That is derived arithmetic, and it means the notches can also punch holes in a tonal Doppler track.

## The matching pipeline inverts flight tracks with a grid search

All of the implementations share one pipeline, which hermes documents most explicitly. Audio is resampled (to 4 kHz in hermes), and steady local hums are notched out. A whitened spectrogram is summed across harmonics, and ridges are tracked with CFAR detection and Viterbi tracking. An event is accepted only if it beats a constant-tone hypothesis on an F-test, shows a plausible speed and has its CPA inside the clip ([hermes](https://github.com/mojomast/hermes-aircraft-doppler-geolocation)). Each accepted event yields (*v*, *R_c*, *t_c*). Historical tracks come mostly from **adsb.lol's daily globe_history archives** (ODbL-licensed, about 3.5–4 GB per day). Jets are filtered out using aircraft-type tables from tar1090-db or ICAO Doc 8643. Terrain comes from Copernicus GLO-30/90 or USGS 3DEP, and temperature and winds aloft from NOAA GFS or Open-Meteo ([dopplertrack](https://github.com/mjshiggins/dopplertrack); [franken_doppler_finder](https://github.com/Dicklesworthstone/franken_doppler_finder); [doppler-locate](https://github.com/digitalis-p/doppler-locate)).

The search then runs forward. For every candidate ground point, it predicts what each nearby prop aircraft would have sounded like there: arrival time, slant distance and speed, using retarded time and the real, possibly turning, track. Candidates must reproduce the measured gap between events, show no other aircraft that should have been heard in between, and then match the full predicted Doppler curve ([cbonitz](https://github.com/cbonitz/doppler-experiments)). cbonitz quantified how hard each filter cuts. The time-offset filter alone keeps **98%** of candidate points. Adding speed and distance leaves **4%**, and the "silence" filter leaves **1.2%** ([cbonitz](https://github.com/cbonitz/doppler-experiments)). Search is typically coarse-to-fine, at 1,000, 200 and 50 m in hermes ([hermes](https://github.com/mojomast/hermes-aircraft-doppler-geolocation)). The unknown *f₀* drops out as a per-event scale factor, and the unknown clock offset is shared across events.

Geometry explains why **two flyovers are the minimum**. One straight pass with an unknown clock constrains the listener to two lines parallel to the track, one on each side, at the horizontal offset `√(R_c² − Δh²)`. A second pass on a non-parallel track, with its timing locked to the first by the measured gap, collapses this to about one point ([cbonitz](https://github.com/cbonitz/doppler-experiments)). The left/right ambiguity of a single pass is broken only by turns. Mirror listeners differ by just 0.7 Hz for a 0.1°/s turn and 6.3 Hz for 1°/s ([cbonitz](https://github.com/cbonitz/doppler-experiments)). Two derived sensitivities matter in practice. A 1 s error in CPA timing at 70 m/s is 70 m of along-track error. Near-overhead passes estimate horizontal offset poorly because `∂d/∂R_c = R_c/d` blows up. That is consistent with dopplertrack's report that two high, nearly overhead passes leave a plateau of candidates **several kilometres wide** ([dopplertrack](https://github.com/mjshiggins/dopplertrack)).

Atmospheric corrections are second-order but measurable. The speed of sound shifts about 0.6 m/s per °C, which scales both the inferred speed and the inferred range. Adding GFS wind and temperature at aircraft altitude cut one doppler-locate residual from **0.55 Hz to 0.21 Hz**. The wind vector fitted from the audio alone (14.4 m/s from 316°) closely matched the GFS value (15.1 m/s from 322°) ([doppler-locate](https://github.com/digitalis-p/doppler-locate)). In other words, the recording also works as a crude anemometer.

## Thirty years of acoustics and forensics anticipated each piece

The flight-parameter science dates to **B. G. Ferguson's 1992 *JASA* paper**. It estimated the altitude and speed of a propeller aircraft from a single ground microphone by least-squares fitting of the Doppler-shifted blade rate ([AIP](https://pubs.aip.org/asa/jasa/article-abstract/92/3/1403/697032/A-ground-based-narrow-band-passive-acoustic?redirectedFrom=fulltext)). Ferguson and Quinn (1994) added short-time Fourier and Wigner–Ville estimators ([Macquarie](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/application-of-the-short-time-fourier-transform-and-the-wigner-vi/)). Reid, Zoubir and Boashash (1997) used polynomial Wigner–Ville distributions with an explicit retarded-time and wind model ([CORE](https://core.ac.uk/download/15120179.pdf)). Ferguson and Lo (2000) extended the work to turboprops and helicopters, using both narrowband and array methods ([AIP](https://pubs.aip.org/asa/jasa/article-abstract/108/4/1763/553561/Turboprop-and-rotary-wing-aircraft-flight)). Patents cover the same ground. US 8,059,489 estimates closest-approach time, slant range and speed from time-frequency curves for airport surveillance ([USPTO](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8059489)), and a DRDO paper does the same for UAVs from one microphone ([DSJ](https://publications.drdo.gov.in/ojs/index.php/dsj/article/download/2238/1198/7489)). All of this prior work solves the **forward problem**: the sensor position is known and the aircraft is unknown. colsto's contribution is to invert it, with the aircraft known from ADS-B and the microphone unknown, searched at continental scale.

Airport noise monitoring already ties microphones to flight tracks, but only coarsely. Systems trigger on threshold events and match them to radar tracks within time and distance windows. Minneapolis–St Paul's MACNOMS validated **92.1%** noise-to-track agreement ([MAC](https://metroairports.org/documents/reports/macnoms-validation-report-2014/direct)), and Airservices Australia sites range from about **67% to 92%** ([Airservices](https://www.airservicesaustralia.com/wp-content/uploads/Short_Term_Monitoring_Program_Tarragindi_QLD_-_Aug_20131.pdf)). AeroSonicDB used ADS-B to automatically trigger and label 625 low-flying aircraft recordings ([arXiv 2311.06368](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.06368)). That kind of dataset is ideal for training the tone detector, which the real-audio results show is the weakest link.

In forensics, the closest analogue is **Electrical Network Frequency (ENF)** analysis. ENF matches the drifting 50/60 Hz mains hum in a recording against a grid-frequency log. The Met Police have logged UK grid frequency since about 2005 and used ENF to authenticate a disputed undercover arms-deal recording ([BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/science-environment-20629671)). ENF answers *when* and *was it edited*, but a synchronous grid is spatially uniform. SWGDE reports experimental location estimates only "on the order of 100 miles" within the US Eastern Interconnect ([SWGDE](https://www.swgde.org/documents/published-complete-listing/09-a-001-swgde-electric-network-frequency-discussion-paper/)). Aircraft-Doppler is, in effect, ENF with a reference that moves and varies in space. The same faint-signal-against-external-log logic yields place as well as time, because every aircraft track is specific to a location.

Gunshot and explosion acoustics are the established OSINT lineage. Earshot and Forensic Architecture measured a crack-to-muzzle-blast gap of about 300 ms in the Shireen Abu Akleh killing and put the shooter about **180 m** from the microphone ([FA/Earshot report](https://content.forensic-architecture.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/2022.11.02_Final-Audio-and-Acoustic-Analysis-SAA-Report.pdf)). Bellingcat and Robert Maher used 76–77 ms gaps to estimate about 45 m in the 2025 Rafah aid-worker killings ([Bellingcat](https://www.bellingcat.com/news/2025/04/09/gaza-palestine-israel-killed-death-aid-workers-international-red-cross-crescent-audio-analysis/)). For Ukraine International Airlines flight **PS752**, Bellingcat converted a 10.7 s flash-to-bang delay into a 3.6 km slant distance and checked it against the Flightradar24 trajectory. That is the clearest precedent for combining acoustic ranging with flight tracks, though it did not use Doppler ([Bellingcat](https://www.bellingcat.com/news/mena/2020/01/09/video-apparently-showing-flight-ps572-missile-strike-geolocated-to-iranian-suburb/)). The nearest published OSINT use of Doppler is Earshot's analysis of the incoming munition's whistle at Gaza's al-Ahli hospital. Earshot inferred the approach direction qualitatively from rising and falling pitch in two videos. US intelligence and the IDF countered that phone audio could not support definitive conclusions, partly because of microphone distortion ([Earshot thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1715381907230232929); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2023/10/20/what-have-open-source-videos-revealed-about-the-gaza-hospital-explosion)). That dispute previews how single-microphone Doppler evidence will be challenged when the stakes are high.

| Technique | Reference matched against | What it yields | Best spatial resolution | Maturity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ENF (mains hum) | Grid-frequency logs | Time, edit detection | Grid level; ~100 miles experimentally | Court use (UK) since ~2005 |
| Crack-bang ballistics | Bullet and sound speeds | Shooter distance | Tens of metres | NGO, newsroom and court use |
| Flash-to-bang + flight track (PS752) | Flightradar24 trajectory | Distance to event | Kilometre scale | One-off Bellingcat analysis |
| Munition Doppler (al-Ahli) | None (track unknown) | Approach direction | Qualitative | Contested |
| Aircraft Doppler + ADS-B | ADS-B archives | Location and absolute time | Tens to hundreds of metres, in the best cases | Days old, unreviewed |

This research found no documented case before November 2026 that matched aircraft audio to ADS-B or Flightradar24 to geolocate or timestamp footage. That is a negative finding from limited searching. Before colsto, aircraft entered verification work as visual objects or track data, and sound entered as a classifier or a ruler. This technique merges the two.

## Real-world accuracy runs from 32 metres to 338 kilometres

All accuracy figures below are self-reported in READMEs written within days of the video. None are independently reviewed. The de facto benchmark is colsto's Oyster Bay clip, and its "ground truth" is an approximate public coordinate, not a surveyed point ([PyPI dopplerprint](https://pypi.org/project/dopplerprint/)).

| Implementation | Test | Reported result | Key caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| colsto (closed) | Oyster Bay CCTV, continental-US search | ~100 m / "a few hundred feet" | Date known; no code or data released |
| doppler-locate | Same challenge recording | Ranked 1st of 1,060 flight pairs, **67 m** | Detector tuned on the same clip; score margin 3.66 vs 3.50 |
| dopplertrack | Oyster Bay; Fort Myers eagle cam; Atlanta rail cam | **32 m** (p = 0.001); 627 m; failed | 2 of 20 searches with no correct answer still reported "located" |
| dopplerprint | 40 single-flyover Livermore recordings | Right aircraft in 34/40; median **207 m**; 27/40 within 500 m | Start time known to ±2 s; 4 km search radius; flyovers hand-marked |
| DOPPLERGEIST | Two real recordings | 43.86 m and 357.47 m | Non-surveyed references; nationwide accuracy "not established" |
| franken_doppler_finder | Own recording; osprey cam; Ithaca feeder | ~260 m; 223 m; **338 km off** | Rural and low-traffic sites all failed |
| doppler-geo | Two-plane Cranfield, UK | Within 40 m | One plane gives only a shortlist |
| hermes | 14 held-out real clips | 1 solved under 3 km; median 12.1 km for solved runs | "Mostly fails on unseen clips" |
| Bodila51 replication | Challenge, automatic detection | Missed by **137 km**; true pair ranked 5th of 517 | Search area chosen knowing the answer |

Sources: [doppler-locate](https://github.com/digitalis-p/doppler-locate); [dopplertrack](https://github.com/mjshiggins/dopplertrack); [PyPI dopplerprint](https://pypi.org/project/dopplerprint/); [VGTimes](https://vgtimes.com/tech-and-hardware/173330-dopplergeist-uses-aircraft-sounds-to-estimate-where-a-recording-was-made.html); [franken_doppler_finder](https://github.com/Dicklesworthstone/franken_doppler_finder); [doppler-geo](https://github.com/teamchong/doppler-geo); [hermes](https://github.com/mojomast/hermes-aircraft-doppler-geolocation); [pasqualepillitteri.it](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/30208/aircraft-noise-geolocates-video-doppler-effect); [cbonitz](https://github.com/cbonitz/doppler-experiments).

Read together, the table supports a firm conclusion. With two clean propeller flyovers, a known date, dense US ADS-B coverage and a well-chosen search region, independent code reaches **tens to a few hundred metres**. That is a genuine confirmation of colsto's core claim. Remove any of those conditions and performance collapses to kilometre-scale misses or confident wrong answers. Headlines promising 100 m "from 60 seconds of audio" rest on a one-minute test in which the recording time was known ([Digg](https://digg.com/ai/7wr4rmyp)). The most worrying entries are dopplertrack's false "located" verdicts and doppler-locate's narrow winning margin. They mean a result should be reported as a confidence region with runner-up candidates, never as a pin. Paras Chopra's reported field test on a Southampton webcam is unverified; only one secondary blog reports it ([ThakiCloud](https://thakicloud.com/tech-blog/en/dev/airplane-doppler-geolocation/)).

## Coverage gaps, edited audio and empty skies defeat it

**Flight data is the binding constraint.** adsb.lol's archive begins in 2023, so older footage, including the 2017 test recordings doppler-locate tried, cannot be located ([doppler-locate](https://github.com/digitalis-p/doppler-locate)). Volunteer coverage is thin in rural areas and at low altitude. Flightradar24 notes that multilateration needs at least three receivers to hear the same aircraft, which typically happens only above 5,000–10,000 ft ([FR24](https://www.flightradar24.com/blog/inside-flightradar24/how-we-track-flights-with-mlat/)). Low-level general aviation is not required to carry ADS-B Out everywhere ([14 CFR 91.225](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/91.225)). Different networks disagree with each other. In doppler-locate, one faint curve had no matching aircraft in adsb.lol, which the author attributes to coverage differences with Flightradar24, so a "no match" is weak evidence against a claimed location ([doppler-locate](https://github.com/digitalis-p/doppler-locate)). Access is also shifting. JETNET bought ADS-B Exchange in 2023, pushing feeders to adsb.lol and airplanes.live ([AIN](https://ainonline.com/aviation-news/business-aviation/2023-01-25/jetnet-buys-ads-b-exchange)), and OpenSky's historical database requires an approved application ([OpenSky docs](https://openskynetwork.github.io/opensky-api/trino.html)). Privacy programmes such as LADD and PIA hide *who* owns an aircraft, not *where* it is, so they barely affect the technique ([NBAA](https://nbaa.org/aircraft-operations/security/privacy/limiting-aircraft-data-displayed-ladd/)).

The method is weakest exactly where conflict OSINT needs it most. EUROCONTROL estimates up to **38% of European en-route traffic** passes through areas intermittently affected by GNSS interference. A single spoofing event left 93 aircraft with ADS-B gaps ([EUROCONTROL via ICAO](https://www.icao.int/sites/default/files/MID/MeetingDocs/2026/GNSS%20%26%20PBN%20Workshop/Documentation/GNSS%20Vulnerabilities%20and%20Emerging%20Threats-EUROCONTROL.pdf)). Military aircraft routinely fly with ADS-B off ([TWZ](https://www.twz.com/tracking-u-s-military-aircraft-online-could-become-much-harder)). A spoofed track is worse than a missing one, because it yields a confidently wrong listener location. Over oceans there are no ground receivers at all.

**The audio side is fragile in different ways.** Propeller tones sit at about 50–600 Hz and are often inaudible. In doppler-locate's tests, pistons were missed at 4–10 km, turboprops beyond about 7 km and almost all jets. Detected passes had distances 12–29% too long, and a Cessna 182 at 3.4 km faded after closest approach, leaving its parameters undetermined ([doppler-locate](https://github.com/digitalis-p/doppler-locate)). The constant-speed, straight-line model is biased by aircraft that climb, descend or slow down, which forces wide tolerances of ±8% on *f₀* ([doppler-locate](https://github.com/digitalis-p/doppler-locate)). Cars, boats, tools and HVAC hums can all mimic aircraft templates. City, indoor and night recordings usually catch nothing, and many webcam archives turned out to have silent audio tracks ([ThakiCloud](https://thakicloud.com/tech-blog/en/dev/airplane-doppler-geolocation/)). The clock matters as much as the sound. The method depends on preserved timing between events plus a known date, or else a costly multi-day search; flyover-locator searches up to 31 days by default ([flyover-locator](https://github.com/amintt2/flyover-locator)). Single-flyover modes remain "implemented but not validated" ([doppler-locate](https://github.com/digitalis-p/doppler-locate)).

## Countermeasures exist, but only blunt ones are proven

Several intuitive defences fail. colsto reports that **high-pass filtering, added background noise and telephone-style compression** all left the aircraft patterns detectable ([IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/inaudible-aircraft-sounds-reveal-recording-location-1829800)). A 200 Hz low-cut test in the Cbonitz replication likewise left the signal intact ([pasqualepillitteri.it](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/30208/aircraft-noise-geolocates-video-doppler-effect)). Stripping GPS metadata does nothing, because the leak is in the ambient audio itself.

What works, roughly in descending order of certainty:

1. Remove or replace the audio with music or voice-over.
2. Cut or splice the footage so the timing between flyovers is lost.
3. Publish later and remove clues to the recording date.
4. Apply aggressive AI noise suppression.
5. Avoid recording under general-aviation or approach paths.

The first three come from [pasqualepillitteri.it](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/30208/aircraft-noise-geolocates-video-doppler-effect), and colsto himself flags cutting and noise cancellation as likely to "make it harder" ([IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/inaudible-aircraft-sounds-reveal-recording-location-1829800)). Defensive tooling has already appeared. Bodila51 published a Claude Code skill that detects and masks flyovers ([pasqualepillitteri.it](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/30208/aircraft-noise-geolocates-video-doppler-effect)). The ENF literature supplies a template: a notch filter erases mains hum ([DSU lecture](https://dsu-digital-sound-design.github.io/dad-498-audio-forensics/lectures/week-6/enf-analysis-and-metadata-consistency)), and a frequency-tracking notch would be the Doppler equivalent.

Two countermeasures are plausible but untested, and both are my own physics inferences. Pitch-shifting alone is probably weak, because the speed estimate depends only on the *ratio* (f₊ − f₋)/(f₊ + f₋) and *f₀* is already a free parameter. Time-stretching is probably stronger, because it distorts both the steepness that encodes range and the gap between flyovers. A 44.1/48 kHz sample-rate mislabel scales both by about 8.8%, comparable to the tools' tolerances.

**The ethics are classic dual use.** Legitimate uses include checking whether a viral video matches its claimed region, tracing the source of leaked footage, and auditing your own livestreams for exposure ([ThakiCloud](https://thakicloud.com/tech-blog/en/dev/airplane-doppler-geolocation/)). The same capability can locate people who deliberately hid where they are: abuse survivors, dissidents in exile, whistleblowers. The added risk is highest for exactly the careful users who have already scrubbed metadata and visual clues. colsto concedes that "easier methods likely exist" for locating most people ([IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/inaudible-aircraft-sounds-reveal-recording-location-1829800)). Even so, the Strava heatmap episode showed how incidental data can expose sensitive sites at scale ([Bellingcat](https://bellingcat.com/resources/how-tos/2018/01/29/strava-interpretation-guide)). Tool authors are self-regulating. doppler-locate restricts use to your own, challenge or consented recordings, and its git hooks block committing precise coordinates. franken_doppler_finder and dopplerprint frame themselves as pre-publication self-audits ([doppler-locate](https://github.com/digitalis-p/doppler-locate); [franken_doppler_finder](https://github.com/Dicklesworthstone/franken_doppler_finder)).

For evidentiary use, the Berkeley Protocol's accuracy and harm-minimisation principles apply directly ([UN Digital Library](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/3973652?ln=en)), as does Koenig's warning about inaccurate conclusions drawn without enough expertise ([AJIL Unbound](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/FE78D42FD4D344F6B1EE98413C20B5DF/S2398772324000023a.pdf/ethical-considerations-for-open-source-investigations-into-international-crimes.pdf)). In practice that means three things: report probabilistic regions with runner-up candidates, preserve the exact ADS-B snapshot used, and corroborate with independent methods. The ENF community took years to establish validated reference databases and known error rates ([AAFS 2013](https://www.aafs.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/AAFS-2013-B25.pdf)), and aircraft-Doppler has neither. Commenters have also raised the reverse threat: generative audio models could insert synthetic flyovers to plant a false location ([Digg](https://digg.com/ai/7wr4rmyp)). Combined with GNSS-spoofed tracks, that makes deliberate deception a live concern. As of 5 November 2026, no documented case of the technique being used against a person or in court was found.

## Conclusion

The episode matters less as a new physics result than as a demonstration of how quickly data abundance turns old military acoustics into a civilian surveillance capability. Ferguson's 1992 single-microphone estimator needed only one inversion: treat the aircraft as known and the microphone as unknown. It also needed a free, crowd-sourced, archived flight database, and that has existed only since about 2023. AI coding agents then compressed reimplementation from months to an afternoon, so a closed-source demo became a public toolkit before any review, error-rate study or safety guidance existed. The realistic threat today is narrow: post-2023, unedited, ambient-audio recordings made outdoors in well-covered, prop-heavy airspace, mostly in the US. Within that envelope, independent code confirms tens-to-hundreds-of-metres accuracy.

Three implications follow. First, "ambient audio is location data" should join metadata and visual landmarks in opsec and journalist-safety guidance, and none of the major guides yet address it. Second, the method is least reliable in conflict zones, where spoofing, dark military traffic and sparse receivers produce confidently wrong answers. Investigators should treat its outputs like early ENF: corroborating evidence with explicit uncertainty, not a standalone fix. Third, the obvious next steps are multi-cue fusion (Lloyd's-mirror fringes for jets, ENF for time) and ML detectors trained on ADS-B-labelled datasets like AeroSonicDB. Each would widen the envelope. Defensive audio scrubbing, rather than relying on coverage gaps, is therefore the durable protection.
