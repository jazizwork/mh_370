# Independent (non-official) MH370 flight-path analyses and the public raw datasets they use

Status as of 5 Nov 2026. Labels used below: **[OFFICIAL]** = government/investigator product (ATSB, DSTG, Malaysian SIR/AAIB); **[PEER-REVIEWED]** = journal publication; **[INDEPENDENT]** = self-published or blog work, not peer-reviewed; **[ADVOCACY/MEDIA]** = journalism or claims by proponents, weak evidence. Many secondary sources on WSPR (AirlineRatings.com in particular) publish Godfrey's own rebuttals and lean partisan, so treat them as proponent material.

## Q1. The Independent Group (IG / UGIB) and Victor Iannello's blog: methods, end points, key findings

### Takeaway
The IG formed in 2014 on Duncan Steel's blog. It reconstructs paths from the Inmarsat BTO/BFO data, using aircraft performance, autopilot-mode and fuel models, and adds drift studies and end-of-flight simulations. Its end points have moved from ~36-37°S (2014-15) to the "IG Hotspot" near 34.2°S 93.8°E (UGIB 2020; 2023 drift study). That hotspot area was part of what Ocean Infinity (OI) searched in 2025-26, and nothing was found. Iannello's blog is now the main clearing-house for this work. In March 2026 it promoted a non-IG hydroacoustic candidate far to the north, near Java (Ed Anderson, 8.36°S 107.92°E).

### Cited Findings
**History and early end points**
- The IG was an informal group of mostly scientists that formed in discussions on Duncan Steel's website. It criticised Inmarsat's early analysis and pushed for the data to be released. [INDEPENDENT] — [Wikipedia: MH370 satellite communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_Airlines_Flight_370_satellite_communications)
- IG statement of 17 June 2014: final location near 36°01′S 88°34′E (6th handshake) — [duncansteel.com archive 826](https://web.archive.org/web/20150323130707/http://www.duncansteel.com/archives/826)
- IG progress report, 26 Sep 2014. It said the complete, unredacted Inmarsat logs (all rows and fields) were needed for BTO/BFO analysis — [duncansteel.com archive 1104](https://web.archive.org/web/20150321124240/http://www.duncansteel.com/archives/1104); [IG Further Progress Report PDF](https://tmfassociates.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/MH370-IG-Further-Progress-Report.pdf)
- Richard Godfrey's Flight Path Model v15.1 (July 2015): ~37°06′18″S 89°52′16″E — [duncansteel.com archive 1874](https://web.archive.org/web/20150919051352/http://www.duncansteel.com/archives/1874#more-1874)
- Simon Hardy, a B777 captain (Mar 2015), modelled a 188° magnetic track ending ~38°04′55″S 87°24′00″E — [Wikipedia: MH370 satellite communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_Airlines_Flight_370_satellite_communications)
- The IG said it could not rule out a northern track on public evidence until the logs were released on 27 May 2014 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_Airlines_Flight_370_satellite_communications)

**UGIB 2020 and later**
- In Feb 2020, Iannello, Bobby Ulich, Godfrey and Andrew Banks released a paper defining a new search area next to previously searched seabed. Their best-estimate final trajectory runs due south from waypoint BEDAX at constant longitude 93.7875°E. [INDEPENDENT] — [AirlineRatings: new search areas](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/new-search-areas-mh370-world-experts)
- Iannello's blog gives the UGIB 2020 Last Estimated Position as 34.23°S 93.78°E (the "IG Hotspot"). A 2023 drift study put the most likely impact near 34.0°S, and the 2023 high-priority area was centred near 34.53°S 93.84°E — [radiantphysics.com home/posts](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/)
- Godfrey's drift analysis gives a common origin of 34.13°S ±1.06° near the 7th arc. The 2023 drift study used CSIRO BRAN2015 drift trials and 17 debris reports — [AirlineRatings](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/new-search-areas-mh370-world-experts); [radiantphysics.com](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/)
- 2025 flaperon photo analysis: barnacle growth suggests the flaperon beached about 2 months earlier than previously assumed. This bears on drift-model latitude — [radiantphysics.com](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/)

**End-of-flight simulations (Boeing data obtained via ATSB)**
- Iannello was given access to Boeing simulation runs made for the ATSB: 10 cases varying speed, altitude, heading and turbulence. In none of them did the aircraft fly wings-level after autopilot disconnect. He recommended searching the 7th arc north of 25°S, ±25 NM wide. The ATSB allowed him to share the results — [AirlineRatings: new analysis](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/new-analysis-possibly-identifies-new-mh370-location); blog post "End-of-Flight Simulations of MH370" (19 Aug 2018) — [radiantphysics.com](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/2018/08/19/end-of-flight-simulations-of-mh370/)
- The final BFO values suggest a steep descent of roughly 0.7 g near the 7th arc — [radiantphysics.com](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/)
- 2019 Penang study: using civilian radar data released in April 2018 and a PMDG 777 in MS Flight Simulator, Iannello's team concluded "It is very unlikely that there was an intention to land at Penang Airport" — [AirlineRatings](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/mh370-pilot-try-deceive-radar-operators)

**Captain's home flight-simulator data (Iannello, 12 Oct 2017)** — [Simulator Data from Computer of MH370 Captain: Part 1](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/2017/10/12/simulator-data-from-computer-of-mh370-captain-part-1/)
- Eight Microsoft FS2004 (FS9) flight-file fragments were recovered from a volume shadow copy on drive "MK25", a drive found disconnected from the computer. Three are on the ground at KLIA and five in flight. Six appear to come from one session on 2 Feb 2014 lasting about 1 hour. That date comes from the ATSB Operational Search report; the date and time values were omitted from the RMP report.
- The points run as follows: KLIA rwy 32R (~68,424 kg fuel inferred) → Malacca Strait (airway R467, 23,247 ft) → toward VAMPI → west of the Andaman/Nicobar Islands (40,003 ft, heading 256°, banked 20° left, turning south) → two points near **45°S 104°E**, 2.5 NM apart, at 37,561 ft and 4,000 ft. Fuel declines to exhaustion. Position and fuel were edited manually during the session. A great circle through the 10°N point and the 45°S point passes McMurdo Station.
- The AFP gave the data to the ATSB on 19 Apr 2014. Large parts of the RMP report were made public after French media obtained it.
- Iannello's caveat: the session shows what was on the captain's computer, not what MH370 did. The ATSB called the simulated track "inconsistent" with the satellite-derived paths — [Gulf News/AP 2016](https://gulfnews.com/world/oceania/mh370-captains-simulator-had-indian-ocean-route-australia-1.1869790)
- The Malaysian transport minister called it one of thousands of simulated routes — [Inquirer/AP](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/800350/australia-mh370-captains-simulator-had-indian-ocean-route)
- Note: the brief named the FBI as the recovering agency. The post does not confirm the FBI's role; early news reports only said the FBI was helping.

**Iannello's critiques of official data**
- The Malaysian SIR (30 Jul 2018) runs to 1,423 pages with appendices, but did not release the raw military radar data needed for independent review — [radiantphysics ?p=920](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/?p=920)
- He argues the SIR ACARS/traffic log was assembled from two reports with different filter parameters — [radiantphysics ?p=985 "MH370 Message Logs Were Edited"](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/?p=985)

**2025-26 search context on the blog**
- Armada 7806 (Mar 2025) filled data gaps, including the high-priority area, then surveyed new areas and left for Singapore in bad weather. Armada 8605, with three Hugin AUVs, began a third campaign on 30 Dec 2025. In Mar 2026 OI and Malaysia paused the IG-Hotspot work, to resume "when circumstances allow" — [radiantphysics posts](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/2026/01/01/the-search-for-mh370-continues-into-2026/); [Update Mar 2025](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/2025/03/31/update-on-the-search-for-mh370/)
- **Ed Anderson acoustic candidate [INDEPENDENT, not peer-reviewed].** The site is 8.36°S 107.92°E, ~42 mi off Java on the 7th arc, with claimed 2-3 km accuracy. A Hugin AUV could cover it in about an afternoon. The evidence:
  - The event originates at 01:15:18 UTC, ~56 min after the last 7th-arc ping (00:19:37).
  - It arrives at Diego Garcia HA08 at 01:59:20.
  - It is also seen on HA01 Cape Leeuwin, on 6 of 7 French hydrophones near Amsterdam Island (University of Brest report LDO-201608, unpublished), on a non-public towed array of more than 6,000 hydrophones near Exmouth, and on 40+ regional seismometers. No earthquake is catalogued for it.
  - Curtin's 2014 analysis (ATSB Final Report, Oct 2018, appendices h and i) had labelled it a small Java Trench earthquake.
  - Iannello: it is "impossible to definitively relate this acoustic event to MH370."
  - The commenter "Viking" argues the energy is orders of magnitude too large for a debris impact, suggests a meteorite, and questions whether a large section could stay afloat for more than 50 minutes before sinking.
  - Anderson's reports are at 370Location.org.
  — [radiantphysics, 10 Mar 2026](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/2026/03/10/a-candidate-site-for-mh370-that-can-be-searched-quickly-and-soon/)

### Inferences
- IG results depend heavily on assumptions about autopilot mode, speed and fuel. The repeated southward/northward shifts (37°S → 35°S → 34.2°S) and the empty 2025-26 hotspot search show that BTO/BFO data alone leave latitude along the arc poorly constrained.
- The simulator 45°S point is evidence about intent, not a measurement of MH370's path. It lies well south of IG estimates.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the full UGIB 2020 paper (the title "The Final Resting Place of MH370" was not confirmed). The 34.23°S latitude comes from Iannello's blog summary only.
- I found no definitive public statement of the exact polygons OI covered in 2025-26.

## Q2. Godfrey's WSPRnet "passive radar" claim, crash point, and critiques/validation

### Takeaway
Godfrey (with Hannes Coetzee and Simon Maskell of the University of Liverpool) claims that anomalies in WSPR amateur-radio SNR and drift records trace MH370 to ~29.128°S 99.934°E. The work is self-published technical reports, not peer-reviewed journal papers. Critics (Iannello, Steve Kent, Metabunk) argue the claimed detection rates are near chance and that the anomalies fit normal ionospheric variability. Maskell's Liverpool validation against known flights was described as in progress, and I found no published results. The 2025-26 OI search reportedly did not target the WSPR point.

### Cited Findings
**The claim and its history**
- The method was first proposed for MH370 by Robert Westphal in July 2020 — [AirlineRatings](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/mh370-new-research-paper-confirms-wsprnet-tracking-technology)
- Godfrey's 2021 WSPR report placed the wreck ~1,993 km west of Perth at 4,000 m depth — [AirlineRatings](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/mh370-new-credible-evidence-location)
- **Aug 2023 report** (Godfrey, Coetzee, Maskell; 229-232 pp.): 125 anomalous WSPR links give 67 positions over 6 h 27 min, with a probable crash site ~1,560 km west of Perth. The authors say it aligns with Boeing/Inmarsat analyses and UWA drift work. Peer-review status is contradictory: one outlet says "peer-reviewed", another says academics were "being asked to peer review" — [Yahoo/AirlineRatings](https://au.news.yahoo.com/location-lost-plane-mh370-revealed-003200199.html); [AirlineRatings](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/mh370-ground-breaking-report-reveals-location)
- **15 Feb 2024 paper "How Does WSPR Detect Aircraft over Long Distances?"** reports 313 anomalies at 130 time points along the path. It states the crash location as **29.128°S 99.934°E**, outside earlier ATSB/OI areas. The paper is hosted on Dropbox, and MATLAB code and data were offered to radio amateurs on request — [mh370search.com ?p=1419](https://www.mh370search.com/?p=1419)
- In Mar 2024, a 30 km radius around that point was promoted — [Borneo Post via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/malaysia/the-borneo-post/20240311/281479281382704); [Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2024/03/10/amateur-investigators-say-confident-mh370s-final-resting-place-lies-west-of-perth/122561)
- A later "refined" position of 29.178850°S 99.85352°E has been quoted — [AirlineRatings](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/new-mh370-location-confirmation)
- Validation case studies by the authors:
  - A Cessna 551 that crashed in the Baltic Sea in 2022 (Maskell's reviewer comments are acknowledged).
  - A short-range (252 km) test near transmitter W8AC (Dec 2023).
  - Long-distance paper (Feb 2024).
  - Maskell was described as validating WSPR against ~1,000 Boeing 777 flights using an ADS-B-referenced processing chain with ROC-curve output.
  — [AirlineRatings short-distance](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/revolutionary-mh370-tracking-proven-over-short-distances); [AirlineRatings long-distance](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/mh370-update-new-report-proves-wsprnet-tracking-over-long-distance); [mh370search.com ?p=1496](https://www.mh370search.com/?p=1496)
- Maskell (2025 interview, via search snippet of The Diplomat, Apr 2025) was quoted as saying "all three explanations remain commensurately likely". The full context was not retrievable (403/paywall) — [The Diplomat](https://thediplomat.com/2025/04/mh370-the-devil-is-in-the-data)

**Critiques**
- **Iannello, on the revised paper:** at a 0.75 threshold, the true-positive rate is 52% and the false-positive rate rises from 37% to 44%, against ~45% detections expected by chance for normally distributed SNR. He called the detector's utility "almost imperceptible." He also argued that the "anomalies" fall within the expected variation of long-distance skywave (ionospheric) propagation — [Metabunk repost "WSPR Can't Find MH370"](https://metabunk.org/goto/post?id=301066); [AirlineRatings rebuttal piece](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/critics-of-wsprnet-tracking-mh370-been-answered)
- **Steve Kent:** "insufficient signal strength, by many orders of magnitude" for WSPR to detect aircraft over long distances — [AirlineRatings rebuttal piece citing Kent](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/critics-of-wsprnet-tracking-mh370-been-answered)
- **Methodology disputes:** the authors discarded anomalies between 0.25 and 0.75 SD, which changed the track, and handled duplicate spots in disputed ways. Godfrey replies that cruise-phase results with duplicates are the same (58%) and that there is an average of 4.3 anomalous links per cruise point — [AirlineRatings](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/critics-of-wsprnet-tracking-mh370-been-answered)
- **Forum critics:** the ROC values are only slightly above random, and some case-study data sit behind a paywall/NDA — [mh370search.com ?p=1496 comments / r/MH370](https://lr.eu.psf.lt/r/MH370/comments/1bguz5w/mentour_pilot_covers_mh370/kvcd683/?context=3)
- Wikipedia notes that Iannello disputed that WSPR could detect the aircraft — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_Airlines_Flight_370_satellite_communications)

**Search outcome relative to WSPR**
- The renewed search ended in Mar 2026 without finding wreckage. A low-reliability site claims it covered the CAPTION zone near 35.7°S 93°E rather than the WSPR hotspot — [4orbs (unverified)](https://4orbs-website.pages.dev/research/ocean-search/)
- Less than half of Godfrey's suggested area had previously been searched — [Substack](https://changingtimes.substack.com/p/ocean-infinity-halts-search-for-mh370)

### Inferences
- WSPR is the clearest precedent for reconstructing a path from incidental public sensor data. Its weaknesses carry over to any similar effort:
  - SNR is quantised to 1 dB.
  - Spots come every 2 minutes, on links not designed for the purpose.
  - Natural ionospheric variance dominates the signal.
  - Validation has to compare against many known flights (ADS-B ground truth) with ROC statistics.
  - The candidate track was chosen with prior knowledge of the Inmarsat arcs, so detections risk confirmation bias.
- 29.1°S lies within the northern part of the latitude band that the Boeing end-of-flight simulations and drift studies allow (north of 25°S was recommended by Iannello). The WSPR result is therefore not physically excluded, but it is not independently confirmed.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed journal publication of the WSPR method or the Liverpool ~1,000-flight validation results was found. Status as of Nov 2026 is unknown.
- I could not confirm whether any part of the 29.1°S area was imaged by OI in 2025-26.

## Q3. Other notable independent theories and analyses

### Takeaway
Besides the IG and WSPR, the main proposals are:
- the Marchand/Blelly piloted-glide hypothesis (CAPTION), which widens the search beyond ±25 NM of the arc;
- Byron Bailey's controlled-ditching-further-south view;
- Jeff Wise's spoofed-BFO northern route to Kazakhstan;
- Florence de Changy's shoot-down/cover-up near Vietnam;
- Ed Anderson's and Usama Kadri's hydroacoustic work.

Debris evidence (Blaine Gibson's finds and others) supports a southern Indian Ocean end and weighs against the northern-route and South China Sea theories.

### Cited Findings
- **Marchand and Blelly (RAeS lecture, 7 Sep 2023):** a deliberate piloted flight ending in a controlled ditching/glide. The last engine was voluntarily shut down so the APU powered the controls during the glide. They proposed an area searchable in ~10 days. OI partly covered it, but per CAPTION not the band 50-70 NM from the 7th arc. CAPTION also reports a possible new search phase between Nov 2026 and Apr 2027. The ATSB final report said descent rates ruled out a controlled glide intended to extend range — [mh370-caption.net](https://mh370-caption.net/); [Blelly trajectory](https://www.mh370-caption.net/index.php/captain-blellys-trajectory/); [The Aviationist, Sep 2023](https://theaviationist.com/2023/09/15/new-theory-mh-370/); [NST Dec 2023](https://nst.com.my/amp/news/nation/2023/12/993969/mh370-could-be-found-days-experts-call-msian-and-australian-govts-begin)
- **Byron Bailey** (Australian ex-pilot/commentator) attacked the ATSB's "nonsensical end-of-flight theory", argued for a controlled ditching further south, and said he would "bet my house on it" — [Yahoo](https://malaysia.news.yahoo.com/aussie-pilot-claims-knows-exactly-021810200.html); [Yahoo SG](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/2-years-wasted-searching-mh370-054321933.html)
- **Jeff Wise** argued the BFO data could have been tampered with by hijackers, giving a northern route to Yubileyniy airstrip (Baikonur, Kazakhstan). An Iannello guest post on Wise's blog concluded that spoofing "does not really add confirmative evidence for the northern routes." Critics note the terrain makes hiding a 777 implausible [ADVOCACY] — [jeffwise.net guest post 2015](https://jeffwise.net/?p=3969); [Tasmanian Times](https://tasmaniantimes.com/?p=55305); [Fear of Landing debunk](https://fearoflanding.com/accidents/pretty-crazy-actually-debunking-the-latest-mh370-solution/)
- **Florence de Changy** (*The Disappearing Act*) argues MH370 was shot down off southern Vietnam/Gulf of Thailand and covered up. Accounts of her location and mechanism differ. Gibson says it "is not supported by a shred of debris evidence"; no debris was found in the South China Sea [ADVOCACY] — [SCMP review](https://m.scmp.com/magazines/post-magazine/books/article/3125808/what-really-happened-flight-mh370-disappearing-act); [AirlineRatings](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/mh370-conspiracy-theories-no-evidence-just-traumatize-relatives)
- **Blaine Gibson** is credited with finding more than half of all known debris. One count lists 43 floating items likely or confirmed from MH370 — [AirlineRatings](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/mh370-sleuth-says-media-reports-are-wrong-on-debris); [mh370search.com ?p=1419 comments](https://www.mh370search.com/?p=1419)
- **Usama Kadri** (Cardiff), *Scientific Reports* 14:10102 (2024) [PEER-REVIEWED]: CTBTO hydrophone data from Cape Leeuwin (HA01) and Diego Garcia (HA08). Within the official time window he found only one relevant signal, at Cape Leeuwin, and none at Diego Garcia. He said he could not find a signal with the certainty needed to launch a new search, and recommended airgun/explosion calibration along the 7th arc. An earlier 2017 *Sci. Rep.* paper by Kadri et al. covers the same data — [Nature PDF](https://nature.com/articles/s41598-024-60529-1.pdf); [Cardiff press release](https://www.cardiff.ac.uk/news/view/2813809-underwater-signals-generated-by-open-sea-airplane-crashes-could-be-key-to-detecting-final-resting-place-of-mh370); [2017 paper](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/105911/1/s41598-017-14177-3.pdf)
- **Official Inmarsat analysis** (Ashton et al., "The Search for MH370", *J. Navigation* 68:1-22, open access, CC-BY) [PEER-REVIEWED/OFFICIAL]: last contact ~34°42′S 93°00′E. It warns of "significant uncertainty" because of sensitivity to frequency errors. Validation used 87 aircraft and 9 earlier 9M-MRO flights. Sensitivity was ±28° heading and ±9° latitude — [DOI 10.1017/S037346331400068X](https://doi.org/10.1017/S037346331400068X); [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_Airlines_Flight_370_satellite_communications)

### Inferences
- The independent field splits into data-driven arc analyses (IG, WSPR, acoustic) and narrative/intent theories (Wise, de Changy, Bailey). Only the former produce searchable coordinates.
- Two hydroacoustic analyses point to different places: Kadri to a Cape Leeuwin signal (inconclusive), Anderson to Java. Neither has calibrated propagation, which is why Kadri's call for 7th-arc calibration shots matters.

### Gaps
- I found nothing substantive on Mike Chillit's analyses. His proposed end point is not documented here.
- I could not fetch Godfrey's GDTAAA (flight-path) tool documentation.
- I could not establish an exact current count of Gibson's finds.

## Q4. Catalogue of publicly downloadable MH370-relevant datasets

### Takeaway
The public record supports independent arc-based modelling well: the Inmarsat log, ATSB/DSTG analyses, drift outputs, Phase 1 bathymetry, the global WSPR database and CTBTO-derived acoustic studies. It is weak on primary sensor data: raw military radar, an unredacted ACARS/SATCOM log with all fields, raw CTBTO waveforms (access restricted), the full simulator files, and full-resolution Phase 2/OI sonar.

### Cited Findings

**1. Inmarsat SATCOM signalling-unit log [OFFICIAL]**
- Released 27 May 2014 by Malaysia's DCA and Inmarsat as a 47-page PDF ("MH370 Data Communication Logs"), with BTO/BFO fields and hourly handshakes.
- Inmarsat said the 47 pages were all the logs it held for the flight. Families and analysts said it was redacted for "readability" and lacked records from earlier flights for comparison.
- Archived copy: [web.archive DCA PDF](https://web.archive.org/web/20160304040818/http://www.dca.gov.my/mainpage/MH370%20Data%20Communication%20Logs.pdf). Coverage: [gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/inmarsat-releases-mh370-satellite-data); [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/now-anyone-can-access-satellite-data-for-mh370); [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/asia/article/1519853/malaysia-and-uk-satellite-firm-release-data-missing-mh370-flight); [CNN](https://edition.cnn.com/2014/05/27/world/asia/malaysia-missing-plane/index.html)
- Caveats:
  - The BTO bias was calibrated from 17 pre-takeoff signals.
  - BFO depends on the oscillator drift model, satellite inclination and the ground-station translation model.
  - The 1st and 7th handshakes are log-on sequences that need special handling.
  - Independent analysts later argued that some logs in the SIR were edited or merged.
  — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_Airlines_Flight_370_satellite_communications); [radiantphysics ?p=985](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/?p=985)

**2. ATSB reports [OFFICIAL, PDF]**
- "MH370 – Definition of Underwater Search Areas" (26 Jun 2014, updated 18 Aug 2014): [archived PDF](https://web.archive.org/web/20140827002931/http://www.atsb.gov.au/media/5243942/ae-2014-054_mh370_-_definition_of_underwater_search_areas_18aug2014.pdf)
- Flight Path Analysis Update (8 Oct 2014): [archived PDF](https://web.archive.org/web/20190918020649/https://www.atsb.gov.au/media/5163181/AE-2014-054_MH370%20-FlightPathAnalysisUpdate.pdf)
- Operational Search Final Report (3 Oct 2017; Oct 2018 version with Curtin acoustic appendices h and i) — referenced by [radiantphysics](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/2017/10/12/simulator-data-from-computer-of-mh370-captain-part-1/) and [radiantphysics 2026](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/2026/03/10/a-candidate-site-for-mh370-that-can-be-searched-quickly-and-soon/)
- ATSB "Aircraft Debris and Drift Modelling" page: [atsb.gov.au/node/2276](https://www.atsb.gov.au/node/2276) (timed out when I tried to fetch it)

**3. DSTG "Bayesian Methods in the Search for MH370" [OFFICIAL, open-access book, 2016]**
- Draft released by JACC on 30 Nov 2015: [jacc.gov.au report002](http://jacc.gov.au/media/reports/2015/december/report002.aspx). Springer chapter listing: [SpringerProfessional](https://www.springerprofessional.de/factual-description-of-accident-and-available-information/10500222)

**4. Malaysian Safety Investigation Report (30 Jul 2018) + appendices [OFFICIAL]**
- 1,423 pages including appendices. It contains radar-derived plots, ACARS traffic logs and SATCOM logs, but not raw military radar. The military radar altitude values were reported to be wrong.
- Civilian radar data were released in April 2018.
- Sources: [radiantphysics ?p=920](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/?p=920); [Yahoo](https://my.news.yahoo.com/malaysian-military-radar-data-mh370-wrong-says-report-033152421.html); [NST 2018 "data incomplete and modified"](https://www.nst.com.my/amp/news/nation/2018/09/407798/mh370-data-incomplete-and-modified-final-report); [AirlineRatings](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/mh370-pilot-try-deceive-radar-operators)
- The 2014 Preliminary Report and 2015 Factual Information (with appendices such as technical log entries and cargo manifest) — [Metabunk copy of preliminary report](https://metabunk.org/attachments/mh370-preliminary-report-pdf.7076)

**5. Captain's simulator data [partially public]**
- Six or eight FS9 data-set fragments are tabulated in the leaked RMP report and in Iannello's 2017 post, plus IG papers (Aug 2016, and Iannello & Guillaume Nov 2016) on Dropbox.
- Caveats: the date/time fields were omitted from the RMP report, the addon (PSS777) data are absent, and the positions were manually edited.

**9. WSPR spots database [public, volunteer-run]**
- **wspr.live** is a ClickHouse mirror of "all spots ever reported to wsprnet.org", reaching back at least to 2008, so it should cover March 2014.
- Table `wspr.rx` fields: id, time, band, rx/tx sign/lat/lon/locator, distance, azimuth, frequency, power, snr, drift, version, code.
- Query endpoint: `https://db1.wspr.live/?query=SELECT ... FORMAT CSV`, at about 20 requests/min. Filter on time and band.
- Caveats:
  - Raw data include duplicates and false spots.
  - The mirror has had sync gaps and reimports (2024 spot loss, March 2025 server failure), and the frequency column was reimported in Oct 2025.
  - No correctness guarantee. Non-commercial use only.
- wsprnet.org also offers monthly CSV archives (not verified here).
- Source: [wspr.live](https://wspr.live/)
- Inference: SNR is integer dB and spots are 2-minute cycles. 2014 station density was far lower than today.

### Inferences
- Any new data source would be fused with the Inmarsat arcs (BTO rings) as the hard constraint. The most valuable public "free" data for an independent acoustic or other passive-sensor study are:
  - CTBTO/IMOS hydrophone recordings around 00:19-01:30 UTC on 8 Mar 2014;
  - regional seismometers (FDSN/IRIS-type networks — a general fact, not sourced here);
  - WSPR spots.

- Raw CTBTO IMS waveforms normally need vDEC/researcher access rather than open download. I did not verify this in this session.

## Q5. What gaps does public data leave that a new data source could close?

### Takeaway
The binding uncertainty is **position along the 7th arc** (latitude roughly 20°S-39°S) and **distance from the arc** (glide vs. spiral dive). The Inmarsat data fix range from the satellite at each handshake but only weakly constrain speed and heading. After 18:22 UTC the path between handshakes is unobserved, and nothing timestamps or locates the impact itself. A new independent source would close the gap if it gave **a time-stamped location at or after 00:19 UTC** (an impact or breakup event) or **cross-arc bearings during 19:41-00:19 UTC**.

### Cited Findings
- Inmarsat sensitivity is ±9° latitude and ±28° heading. The authors warn of "significant uncertainty" — [Wikipedia summary of Ashton et al.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_Airlines_Flight_370_satellite_communications)
- Boeing simulations allow ±25 NM from the arc, and probably north of 25°S per Iannello. Marchand/Blelly argue 50-70 NM if piloted — [AirlineRatings](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/new-analysis-possibly-identifies-new-mh370-location); [CAPTION](https://mh370-caption.net/)
- Drift studies disagree on latitude: GEOMAR says equatorward of 30°S, the IG says ~34°S, and CSIRO says ~35°S — [radiantphysics ?p=297](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/?p=297); [radiantphysics](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/)
- Hydroacoustic work so far is inconclusive. Kadri asks for calibration shots along the 7th arc. Anderson's Java event can't be tied to MH370 — [Cardiff](https://www.cardiff.ac.uk/news/view/2813809-underwater-signals-generated-by-open-sea-airplane-crashes-could-be-key-to-detecting-final-resting-place-of-mh370); [radiantphysics 2026](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/2026/03/10/a-candidate-site-for-mh370-that-can-be-searched-quickly-and-soon/)
- The search to Mar 2026 covered ~7,571 km² of ~15,000 km² with no finding. Malaysia extended OI's agreement to 30 Jun 2027 for the remaining ~7,428.54 km², with non-continuous operations — [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/03/09/no-trace-of-flight-mh370-in-new-search); [IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/malaysia-extends-mh370-search-until-2027-1805672)
- Withheld raw military radar data stop independent checking of the pre-18:22 path and the turn timing — [radiantphysics ?p=920](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/?p=920)

### Inferences
- The highest-value new data would:
  - independently time an impact on the 7th arc, e.g. a hydroacoustic/seismic event with a known propagation model, which turns a 1-D arc ambiguity into a point;
  - or independently measure ground speed and track during the cruise, e.g. passive RF, which WSPR attempts with poor SNR statistics.
- Any such source must be validated blind against known aircraft or calibration events (ROC curves, false-alarm rates), not tuned on MH370 alone. The WSPR dispute shows that an unvalidated method with a priori arc knowledge invites confirmation bias.
- Public CTBTO and IMOS hydrophone coverage of the southern Indian Ocean in 2014 is sparse: two IMS stations within range, plus duty-cycled IMOS recorders. Bearing-only fixes from one or two stations, combined with BTO arc intersection, may be the realistic ceiling.

### Gaps
- No calibration experiment (airgun/explosion) along the 7th arc has been reported as of Nov 2026.
- Neither the Liverpool WSPR validation results nor OI's coverage polygons are public, so the two leading independent hypotheses cannot be scored against the empty search.
