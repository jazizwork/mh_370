# MH370 end point from floating debris: reverse drift modelling and barnacle (biological) evidence

Notes as of 5 Nov 2026. Each finding carries an inline source. "Inferences" are my reasoning, not sourced fact. Several numbers come from secondary coverage or from summaries of fetched pages, and those are flagged. Where sources conflict, both are given.

## 1. Debris catalogue: items, find dates and locations, official assessments, Gibson finds, and what is published

### Takeaway
The official tally, in Malaysia's 2018 Annex 13 Safety Investigation Report (SIR), is 27 significant items examined. 3 are confirmed: the flaperon, part of the right outboard flap and a section of the left outboard flap. Of the rest, 7 are "almost certainly", 8 "highly likely", 1 "likely" and 8 unidentifiable. Independent researchers count more, about 33 items, or about 60 according to Gibson. Since the Annex 13 team was disbanded in 2019, nothing new has been officially assessed, so items found after 2018 have only amateur analysis. Drift studies use only a subset: 17 to 18 items with reliable dates and locations.

### Cited Findings
**Official tallies**
- SIR (2018) tally as reported in the press: 27 significant pieces recovered and examined. 3 confirmed (the Réunion flaperon, part of the right outboard flap, a section of the left outboard flap), 7 "almost certainly" (including some cabin interior items), 8 "highly likely", 1 "likely", 8 not identifiable — [AirlineRatings, "New Detailed Analysis Confirms MH370 Debris"](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/new-detailed-analysis-confirms-mh370-debris) (secondary coverage of the SIR; I could not fetch the SIR table itself).

### Inferences
- Debris evidence is fragmented. The official, peer-reviewable catalogue stops at the 2018 SIR, and later items are assessed only from photos by independent analysts. An independent researcher should build a machine-readable table (item ID, find date, find-date uncertainty, reporting delay, GPS, assessment class, barnacle presence and size, photo URL) from the SIR appendix, the ATSB reports and Godfrey's 2021 paper, and should version it.
- Find dates are not beaching dates. Items like "NO STEP 2" were recognised long after they washed up, so a likelihood model should treat find time as at or after beaching, with a reporting-delay distribution.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the SIR's item-by-item debris table (Appendix 1.12) or the individual ATSB debris examination reports. Item numbering, exact GPS points and the official photos are therefore unverified here.
- Name of the flaperon finder: not in the pages I fetched.
- I found no official status for any item found after 2018.
- I found no consolidated, machine-readable public debris dataset with EXIF/GPS metadata.

## 2. Drift-modelling studies: conclusions, methods, differences, and 2023–2026 updates

### Takeaway
Published inverse-drift estimates fall into two families:
- **"Southern" (~34–36°S).** CSIRO/ATSB 2016–17 (most likely 35.6°S 92.8°E), Godfrey 2021 (34.13°S ± 1.06°) and Ulich & Iannello 2023 (34.0°S, plausible 30–36°S). These use flaperon-specific leeway measured on a real B777 flaperon, the absence of debris on the WA coast, and calibration against GDP drifters.
- **"Northern" (~25–30°S).** GEOMAR 2015–16 (equatorward of ~30°S), Nesterov 2018 (25.5–30.5°S), Miron et al. 2019 (~25°S) and Godfrey's 2017–18 work (~30°S, as far north as 20.5°S). These rely on generic particles or drifter-climatology Markov chains, or on early barnacle temperatures.
- **Spanning both.** CMCC/Jansen 2016 gives 28–35°S.

I found no new peer-reviewed debris-drift end-point study from 2024 to 2026.

### Cited Findings
**CSIRO / ATSB (David Griffin et al.)**
- First report: *The search for MH370 and ocean surface drift*, December 2016, to the ATSB. It underpinned the First Principles Review's recommendation of ~35°S. The ATSB (Dec 2016) put the most likely area at about 34°S 93°E, covering ~25,000 km² — [CSIRO news, 21 Apr 2017](https://www.csiro.au/en/news/All/Articles/2017/April/mh370); [Wikipedia: Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_for_Malaysia_Airlines_Flight_370).
- Part II: Griffin, Oke & Jones, *The search for MH370 and ocean surface drift – Part II*, CSIRO report EP172633, 13 April 2017 — [ATSB PDF](https://www.atsb.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-02/mh370_ocean_driftii_final.pdf).
- Part II methods:
  - Satellite altimetry-derived surface currents.
  - A global ocean model.
  - Hundreds of NOAA GDP drifters. Drifters that had lost their drogues were treated as analogues of floating aircraft debris.
  - About 20 years of drifter data used for calibration.
  - Replicas built from Boeing drawings and French data.
  - A real B777 flaperon, obtained in Feb 2017 and cut down to match MH370's. Its buoyancy was tank-matched to the French DGA results, then it was field-tested for 13 days in North West Bay and Storm Bay, Tasmania, beside replicas and drifters.
  — [CSIRO news](https://www.csiro.au/en/news/All/Articles/2017/April/mh370)
- Key Part II result: the real flaperon drifts about 20° to the left of the wind and faster than the replicas. This resolved the earlier finding that a July 2015 arrival at Réunion was "too early but possible". Simulated flaperon tracks head to Réunion, not Australia — [CSIRO news](https://www.csiro.au/en/news/All/Articles/2017/April/mh370); [FlightGlobal](https://flightglobal.com/new-flaperon-drift-tests-verify-mh370-likely-resting-place/123776.article).
- Debris found in Africa but not on the WA coast was used as a negative constraint that narrows the latitude range — [CSIRO news](https://www.csiro.au/en/news/All/Articles/2017/April/mh370).
- Part III (Aug 2017): back-tracking of objects in Airbus Pléiades-1A images taken 23 Mar 2014. Geoscience Australia classed 12 objects as "probably man-made" and 28 as "possibly man-made". CSIRO back-tracked them to 8 Mar 2014 using daily altimetry currents, over a 2-week window.
  - Most likely location: **35.6°S, 92.8°E**, stated "with unprecedented precision and certainty", but "not a unique location".
  - Alternatives: 34.7°S 92.6°E and 35.3°S 91.8°E.
  - Caveat: "almost no in-situ observations" exist in the area.
  — [CSIRO news, Aug 2017](https://www.csiro.au/en/news/all/articles/2017/august/satellite-images-add-to-weight-of-evidence-locating-missing-mh370)
- The CSIRO crash area (35°36′S 92°48′E) lies northeast of the main 120,000 km² search zone. The ATSB final report (Oct 2017) says images plus drift narrowed the area to ~25,000 km² — [Wikipedia: MH370](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_Airlines_Flight_370).
- The Australian Transport Minister said the April 2017 report "did not provide new evidence leading to a specific location" — [search summary of press coverage, Apr 2017](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/mh370-csiro-report-boosts-confidence-in-wreckage-location).

**GEOMAR (Jonathan Durgadoo, Arne Biastoch, Kiel)**
- Nearly 2 million virtual particles were back-tracked over the ~16 months from disappearance to the flaperon find. Stokes drift was added in the later analysis. 2015 result: all particles came from equatorward of ~30°S, so the search "might be too far south" — [GEOMAR, "Where is MH370?"](https://www.geomar.de/en/news/article/where-is-mh370); [GEOMAR press release PDF 2015](https://www.geomar.de/fileadmin/news_import/pm_2015_44_MH370-e_01.pdf).
- The May 2016 update, *Backtracking of the MH370 flaperon from La Réunion*, shifted the source region somewhat south but kept "most particles north of the search area". GEOMAR said the uncertainties were "very large" — [GEOMAR May 2016 report PDF](https://www.geomar.de/fileadmin/content/service/presse/Pressemitteilungen/2016/MH370_Report_May2016-klein.pdf); [GEOMAR "MH370: where to search"](https://geomar.de/en/news/article/mh370-where-to-search).
- A secondary blog cites "98.7% probability north of the seabed search area". Unverified against the report — [Jeff Wise blog](https://jeffwise.net/2016/05/11/new-mh370-study-supports-shift-of-seabed-search/comment-page-5).
- Follow-up methods paper: Durgadoo et al. (2019), "Strategies for simulating the drift of marine debris", *Journal of Operational Oceanography* (per search summary; DOI not verified). A related abstract is [EGU2017-7553](https://opac.geologie.ac.at/ais312/dokumente/EGU/EGU2017-7553.pdf).

**NOAA AOML / Trinanes et al. 2016**
- Trinanes, Olascoaga, Goni, Maximenko, Griffin & Hafner, "Analysis of flight MH370 potential debris trajectories using ocean observations and numerical model results", *J. Operational Oceanography* 9(2):126–138, 2016, [doi:10.1080/1755876X.2016.1248149](https://doi.org/10.1080/1755876X.2016.1248149) — [AOML PDF](https://www.aoml.noaa.gov/phod/docs/MH370_Trinanes_etal.pdf); [NOAA repository](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/17681/noaa_17681_DS1.pdf).
- Method: GDP drifter trajectories plus model surface currents, run forward and backward. The paper shows high-probability areas and a general westward drift consistent with the African and Mascarene finds. It does not fix a crash site — [Phys.org, Jan 2017](https://phys.org/news/2017-01-oceanographic-analysis-potential-site-mh370.html); [AOML submission draft](https://www.aoml.noaa.gov/ftp/phod/trinanes/JOO/Submission_TJOO-2016-0007_R1.pdf).

**CMCC Italy / Jansen et al. 2016**
- Superensemble drift simulation driven by currents and winds from 14 Mar 2014, with a range of wind-drag coefficients and many perturbed start points. Result: **28–35°S** — [Jansen et al., NHESS 16:1623 (2016)](https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/16/1623/2016/); [The Conversation, 27 Jul 2016](https://theconversation.com/new-research-points-to-the-crash-site-of-missing-plane-mh370-63019).
- University of Hawaii (Maximenko/Hafner) also ran a marine-debris drift model of crash-site flow paths — [SOEST](https://www.soest.hawaii.edu/soestwp/?p=8378) (not read in detail).

**Nesterov 2018**
- "Consideration of various aspects in a drift study of MH370 debris", *Ocean Science* 14:387–402. Screens 40 hypothetical crash sites along the 7th arc against three constraints: aerial-search efficacy (no debris seen), flaperon water temperatures from barnacles (24 → 18 → 25 °C) and the distribution of beached debris. Result: **25.5–30.5°S, with 28–30°S "most promising"** — [Ocean Science](https://os.copernicus.org/articles/14/387/2018/).

**Miron et al. 2019**
- Miron, Beron-Vera, Olascoaga & Koltai, "Markov-chain-inspired search for MH370", *Chaos* 29:041105, [doi:10.1063/1.5092132](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.5092132), [arXiv:1903.06165](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.06165).
- Method: a transition matrix from **undrogued GDP drifters**, three seasonal models for the monsoon, spectral analysis, Bayesian estimation and most-probable paths from the arc to the beaching sites. Result: **~25°S** on the arc — [U. Miami news](https://news.miami.edu/rosenstiel/stories/2019/05/study-suggests-crash-location-of-mh370-near-25degree-south-north-of-underwater-search-area.html); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1903.06165).

**Richard Godfrey / Independent Group (IG) and Ulich & Iannello**
- Godfrey, 14 Feb 2017, "The Probable End Point of MH370": GDP-based, near **30°S** — [Radiant Physics papers](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/papers/).
- Godfrey 2018: 96 GDP drifters, daily averages on a 100 km grid. East African debris could have come from as far north as **20.5°S**, and he recommended searching 25–20°S. Mean drifter speeds were 0.535 kn for all 96 and 0.522 kn for the 18 undrogued drifters, which he called "no significant difference" — [Radiant Physics, 20 Jul 2018](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/2018/07/20/godfrey-drift-model-says-mh370-might-have-crashed-further-north-on-arc); [AirlineRatings](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/new-mh370-study-suggests-north).
- Pattiaratchi said it was "highly unlikely" the debris came from that far north — [AirlineRatings](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/new-mh370-study-suggests-north).
- Godfrey 2021, "MH370 Floating Debris – Drift Analysis" (126 pp.), with drift-modelling help from David Griffin. Of 33 items, 18 were used. Common origin **34.13°S ± 1.06°**, within ~120 km of the UGIB Last Estimated Position. The coverage says nine other drift analyses (Australia, Germany, Italy, USA, China) all point to 28–40°S, mostly 34–35°S. Pattiaratchi's work supports 32–34°S — [AirlineRatings](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/mh370-new-credible-evidence-location); [mh370search.com](https://www.mh370search.com/2021/01/21/mh370-debris-drift-analysis/).
- Ulich & Iannello, June 2023, "Improved Prediction of MH370 Crash Location Based on Drift Modeling of Floating Debris":
  - Inputs: two sets of **86,400 CSIRO BRAN2015 drift trials**, one using flaperon parameters from the sea trials and one for generic debris at 1.2% windage (varied), started within 25 km of Arc 7 between 8°S and 44°S.
  - Statistics: 17 debris reports; Bayesian treatment of BRAN2015 track-localisation error; min/max reporting delays from barnacle encrustation; maximum-likelihood PDF; non-blind, partially-blind and blind validation.
  - Result: **34.0°S**, ~76 km NE of the 2020 UGIB LEP (34.23°S 93.79°E). Plausible origin 30–36°S.
  - Not covered on the blog page: beaching/detection probability and the WA-absence constraint.
  — [Radiant Physics, June 2023](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/2023/06); [paper PDF (Dropbox)](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/5papw6sl0bwo46122dv58/2023-06-12-MH370-Drift-Study-Paper.pdf?rlkey=1px48ktdnzddnw1m97tqbqlqt&dl=0).
- UGIB 2020 (Ulich, Godfrey, Banks), "The Final Resting Place for MH370": LEP **34.2342°S 93.7875°E**. Drift is one input among several — [Radiant Physics papers](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/papers/).

**2024–2026**
- I found no new peer-reviewed debris-drift end-point paper.
- A March 2025 ResearchGate "MH370 Debris Analysis & Drift Simulation Report" exists. Authorship and review status are unclear — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390127570_MH370_Debris_Analysis_Drift_Simulation_Report).
- An Indian Defence Review article claims a May 2024 *Scientific Reports* barnacle/drift study — [IDR](https://indiandefencereview.com/mh370-mystery-may-finally-be-cracked-key-clue-piece-wreckage/). A targeted search found no such paper, so this is likely a garbled reference to Al-Qattan/Herbert 2023. Treat it as unreliable.

### Inferences
- **The main reason the families split is how leeway is treated.**
  - Studies that measured or used real-flaperon leeway (fast, ~20° left of the wind) and calibrated against undrogued GDP drifters land at 34–36°S: CSIRO, Ulich & Iannello, Godfrey 2021.
  - Studies using generic particles, pure-current or Stokes drift, or drifter-only Markov chains land further north: GEOMAR, Miron, Godfrey 2017/18.
  - Studies that used the early, warm-biased barnacle temperatures (Nesterov) also land north.
  - A flaperon that drifts faster than generic debris needs a more southerly or distant origin to arrive at Réunion by July 2015.
- The WA-coast non-detection constraint is strong. CSIRO used it qualitatively; I found no study that formalises it as a likelihood term with a coastal search-effort model.
- The northern family's end points (~25–30°S) are mostly outside the deep-tow and AUV coverage centred on 33–39°S. That makes them the main "unsearched-but-drift-plausible" alternative, though they conflict with the satellite and fuel analyses (see section 5).

### Gaps
- Full texts I did not read: CSIRO Part I (Dec 2016) and Part III, the Durgadoo 2019 paper, and the Jansen 2016 details. Exact windage and leeway values for each study are therefore not tabulated here.
- I could not confirm the claimed "2023 Godfrey ~34.0°S". The 2023 34.0°S result is Ulich & Iannello, and Godfrey's figure is the 2021 34.13°S.
- Whether CSIRO's 86,400-trial BRAN2015 dataset is publicly downloadable is not verified.
- Pattiaratchi's (UWA) own paper and figures were not fetched. The 32–34°S figure is via AirlineRatings.

## 3. Barnacles and biological evidence: Al-Qattan, Herbert et al. 2023, De Deckker, and follow-ups

### Takeaway
Al-Qattan, Herbert et al. (AGU Advances, 2023):
- produced the first lab-calibrated SST–δ¹⁸O equation for *Lepas anatifera*;
- found that earlier calibrations ran several degrees warm;
- reconstructed a partial flaperon drift path that ran **further south** than prior isotope-based work, over its last ~5 months only.

The crash-site latitude cannot be fixed until the largest and oldest barnacles, held by French authorities, are released. I found no published 2024–2026 follow-up that analyses those barnacles.

### Cited Findings
**Paper and authors**
- Al-Qattan, N., Herbert, G.S., Spero, H.J., McCarthy, S., McGeady, R., Tao, R., & Power, A.-M. (2023), "A Stable Isotope Sclerochronology-Based Forensic Method for Reconstructing Debris Drift Paths With Application to the MH370 Crash", *AGU Advances* 4(4), e2023AV000915, online 23 Aug 2023, [doi:10.1029/2023AV000915](https://doi.org/10.1029/2023AV000915). Open access. Institutions: USF, UC Davis, University of Galway — [AGU Advances full text](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2023AV000915).

**Lab calibration**
- 46 live juvenile *L. anatifera* (45 analysed), collected on driftwood at Fanore Beach, Ireland. Acclimated at 12 °C, then grown at **14, 20 and 26 °C** for 3 weeks. New growth was marked with tetracycline.
- IRMS precision: <0.06‰ (calcite) and <0.07‰ (water).
- Reported relation: δ¹⁸O_shell − δ¹⁸O_water = −0.22·T + 27.67, with a 0.27‰ VSMOW/VPDB correction. As extracted by a page-parsing tool; verify the constant in the paper.
- [AGU Advances](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2023AV000915)

**MH370 application**
- The isotope profile used is from a small (<28 mm capitulum) flaperon barnacle designated **A2-G1** in Blamart & Bassinot (2016), the French analysis — [AGU Advances](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2023AV000915).
- That profile was turned into a dated SST series and matched against **50,000 simulated SST histories**. The simulations used ICHTHYOP v3.3 particle tracking on HYCOM (Global Reanalysis expt 53.x), with surface-only particles. Simulated flaperons started on 25 Feb 2015, so the reconstruction covers only the last ~5 months before the find — [AGU Advances](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2023AV000915); [EurekAlert release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/793489).
- Windage was treated as negligible because the flaperon floats near-horizontal and slightly submerged. The paper notes the flaperon can pitch end-over-end in high winds — [AGU Advances](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2023AV000915).
- Result: the reconstructed path spent its last 5 months **west of 70°E, south of 20°S, within 1,500 km of Réunion**. This is "the first confirmation of a more southerly drift track" — [AGU Advances](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2023AV000915).
- Press coverage describes early growth at ~26–27 °C, then a shift to ~23–24 °C — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2023/08/230823122608.htm); [Eos editor highlight](https://eos.org/editor-highlights/barnacles-help-reconstruct-drift-path-of-malaysia-airlines-flight-mh370).
- The paper says "years of failure suggest other areas should be considered" along the 7th arc — [AGU Advances](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2023AV000915).

**Comparison with earlier calibrations**
- Blamart & Bassinot (2016): an uncontrolled calibration from museum specimens and multi-year monthly SSTs, SE ~±2 °C, which predicts temperatures several degrees too warm.
- Mesaglio et al. (2021): differs from Blamart & Bassinot by up to 5 °C.
- [AGU Advances](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2023AV000915)

**Limitations stated in the paper**
- A single SST has no unique spatial solution.
- Error arises if individual growth rates differ from Poupin's average growth curve.
- No sensitivity analyses were done, because the paper is a methods demonstration.
- Profiles differ slightly between barnacles.
- **"Only partial drift reconstructions are possible until the largest, oldest barnacles are released for study by the French government."**
- [AGU Advances](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2023AV000915)
- Herbert, in press coverage, says the largest barnacles may have colonised the flaperon soon after the crash and near the crash site, but had not been made available — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2023/08/230823122608.htm); [AeroTime](https://www.aerotime.aero/articles/barnacles-could-provide-clues-to-mh370-location-scientist-says).

**Data**
- Numerical modelling data: Herbert (2023), USF, V4, [doi:10.17632/nr4vt4h8gt.4](https://doi.org/10.17632/nr4vt4h8gt.4) (Mendeley Data). The rest is in the paper's Supporting Information S1 — [AGU Advances](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2023AV000915).
- UC Davis coverage: [researchers create oceanic tracking model, urge investigators to revive search](https://lettersandsciencemag.ucdavis.edu/science-technology/researchers-create-oceanic-tracking-model-urge-investigators-revive-search) (not read).

**De Deckker (ANU) and earlier barnacle work**
- De Deckker used laser ablation along the shell, with ~20 µm spots and ~1,500 analyses (Mg/Ca). As reported, the temperature history was ~24 °C at the start, a long period at ~18–20 °C, then ~25 °C — [Jeff Wise blog, Sep 2016](https://jeffwise.net/2016/09/10/fascinatingly-mysterious-new-flaperon-barnacle-data/comment-page-4).
- Nesterov (2018) used 24 → 18 → 25 °C as a constraint — [Ocean Science](https://os.copernicus.org/articles/14/387/2018/).
- Al-Qattan et al. note that De Deckker (2017) himself found **no reliable environmental signal in Mg/Ca** of *L. anatifera* and flagged growth-rate error — [AGU Advances](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2023AV000915).
- Barnacle age is disputed: one expert says a couple to ~4 months, a Malaysian source says 6–12 months — [Jeff Wise blog](https://jeffwise.net/2016/09/10/fascinatingly-mysterious-new-flaperon-barnacle-data/comment-page-4) (secondary).

**Growth-rate evidence (Mesaglio et al. 2021, UNSW, *Marine Biology*)**
- *Lepas anserifera* grew up to ~1.45 mm/day, ~1.05 mm/day on average. This implies the 36 mm Lepas on the flaperon could have settled later than assumed, i.e. ~a month of growth rather than many months — [UNSW news](https://www.unsw.edu.au/newsroom/news/2021/02/could-a-common-barnacle-help-find-missing-persons-lost-at-sea-); [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2021-02-common-barnacle-persons-lost-sea.html).

**2025–26 claim (unverified)**
- An independent analyst claims "2025 photo analysis of the flaperon shows the barnacles are growing on top of abrasions from beaching". That would shift the assumed arrival about 2 months earlier — Ed Anderson, comment at [Radiant Physics, 10 Mar 2026](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/2026/03/10/a-candidate-site-for-mh370-that-can-be-searched-quickly-and-soon/). Not peer-reviewed.

### Inferences
- **Herbert's drift simulations conflict with CSIRO's field results.** Herbert assumed negligible windage, while CSIRO's field trials found the real flaperon moves ~20° left of the wind and faster than replicas. Re-running the Herbert SST-matching with CSIRO-style flaperon leeway, and with several reanalyses (HYCOM, GLORYS12, BRAN2020), would test how robust the southerly result is.
- **The warm bias in older calibrations would have pushed earlier reconstructions north.** This is consistent with Nesterov's 25.5–30.5°S, which relied on the De Deckker / early temperatures. With a cooler calibration, barnacle evidence no longer clearly favours the north.
- **The 7th arc spans a large SST range.** It runs from ~20 to ~26 °C at 25°S down to ~14 to 18 °C at 35–39°S in March (my estimate, not sourced). A barnacle that settled in the first weeks could therefore discriminate latitude strongly. This is why access to the oldest shells matters.
- **Barnacle size also bounds reporting delays.** Ulich & Iannello used this. Mesaglio's faster growth rates tighten the bounds and should be propagated into drift likelihoods.

### Gaps
- I found no public statement from French judicial authorities (the Paris investigating judges hold the flaperon) on releasing the barnacles, and no 2024–2026 Herbert follow-up publication.
- The original De Deckker report or paper (2016/2017) was not located as a primary source. The account is via Jeff Wise and Nesterov.
- Barnacle evidence from other items (e.g. Pemba flap, "Roy") was not found in the sources reviewed.

## 4. Open data and tools to reproduce or improve the debris inversion

### Takeaway
Every published method can be rebuilt with free data:
- GDP drifters (including drogue-loss flags);
- free-sign-up reanalysis currents: CMEMS GLORYS/altimetry, HYCOM, BRAN, OSCAR;
- ERA5 winds and waves;
- open Lagrangian frameworks: OpenDrift (leeway and windage, backward runs) and Parcels;
- the Herbert Mendeley dataset.

The clear untried step is a single Bayesian inversion that jointly uses every item's find date and place, non-detections, beaching and reporting probabilities, flaperon-specific leeway, and the isotope SST series, run as an ensemble over several current products.

### Cited Findings
**Data and tools used in the literature**
- GDP drifters: CSIRO used hundreds of drifters, treating drogue-lost ones as debris analogues, with ~20 years of data — [CSIRO](https://www.csiro.au/en/news/All/Articles/2017/April/mh370). Trinanes et al. used GDP plus model currents — [AOML PDF](https://www.aoml.noaa.gov/phod/docs/MH370_Trinanes_etal.pdf). Miron et al. built Markov chains from undrogued GDP drifters — [arXiv:1903.06165](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.06165). Godfrey used 96 GDP drifters — [Radiant Physics 2018](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/2018/07/20/godfrey-drift-model-says-mh370-might-have-crashed-further-north-on-arc).
- HYCOM Global Reanalysis expt 53.x with ICHTHYOP v3.3 and 50,000 particles was used by Al-Qattan/Herbert — [AGU Advances](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2023AV000915). Their model data is at [doi:10.17632/nr4vt4h8gt.4](https://doi.org/10.17632/nr4vt4h8gt.4).
- BRAN2015 (Bluelink reanalysis, CSIRO/BoM) was used for the 2 × 86,400 trials in Ulich & Iannello — [Radiant Physics, June 2023](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/2023/06).
- Stokes drift was included in GEOMAR's later backtracking — [GEOMAR](https://www.geomar.de/en/news/article/where-is-mh370).
- Wind-drag coefficient ranges were used in the CMCC superensemble — [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/new-research-points-to-the-crash-site-of-missing-plane-mh370-63019).

**Free data sources** (project pages; access terms as I understand them, not re-verified this session)
- NOAA GDP drifters, 6-hourly and hourly products with drogue-loss dates. Free, no sign-up — [NOAA AOML GDP](https://www.aoml.noaa.gov/phod/gdp/).
- Copernicus Marine (CMEMS): GLORYS12 reanalysis, multi-mission altimetry geostrophic currents, WAVERYS Stokes drift. **Free self-service sign-up** — [marine.copernicus.eu](https://marine.copernicus.eu/).
- HYCOM global reanalysis and analysis via THREDDS/OPeNDAP. Free — [hycom.org](https://www.hycom.org/).
- OSCAR surface currents via NASA PO.DAAC. **Free NASA Earthdata login** — [PO.DAAC](https://podaac.jpl.nasa.gov/).
- ERA5 hourly 10 m winds and wave parameters via the Copernicus Climate Data Store. **Free sign-up** — [CDS](https://cds.climate.copernicus.eu/).

**Lagrangian frameworks** (both open-source Python)
- OpenDrift: includes a Leeway model with object classes and wind-drift factors, and supports backward runs — [opendrift.github.io](https://opendrift.github.io/).
- OceanParcels: supports custom kernels for windage, Stokes drift and beaching, and backward-in-time advection — [oceanparcels.org](https://oceanparcels.org/).

**Barnacle-specific tools**
- The approach needs isotope profiles, an SST product (e.g. OSTIA/CMEMS or NOAA OISST) along simulated tracks, and a growth-rate model (Poupin curve; Mesaglio rates) — [AGU Advances](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2023AV000915); [UNSW](https://www.unsw.edu.au/newsroom/news/2021/02/could-a-common-barnacle-help-find-missing-persons-lost-at-sea-).

### Inferences
These are my suggested methods. None is drawn from a source.

**A. Reproduce first**
- (i) Rebuild Miron-style seasonal Markov transition matrices from undrogued GDP drifters for 2014–2016 and climatology. Check that you get the ~25°S result, then add a flaperon-leeway correction and see whether it moves south.
- (ii) Seed OpenDrift Leeway or Parcels forward ensembles every ~0.25° along the 7th arc (8–44°S) on 8 Mar 2014. Force them with ≥3 current products (GLORYS12, HYCOM, BRAN2020, OSCAR or altimetry + Ekman) and ERA5 winds/Stokes. Use two windage classes: flaperon-like (fast, ~20° left, per CSIRO) and generic 1–3%.

**B. Then improve with a joint Bayesian likelihood**
- For each item: P(beach in coastal cell c during window [t_beach_min, t_find]) × P(detect | beach) × P(report delay), with delay bounds from barnacle size and growth rate.
- Non-detections: WA coast, no finds before Jul 2015, Mascarene/Madagascar reporting effort over time, and the March–April 2014 aerial-search null (as in Nesterov).
- The A2-G1 isotope SST series as a track-level likelihood.
- Satellite-image objects (CSIRO Part III) as an optional term.
- Use model ensemble spread as structural error; Ulich & Iannello did a Bayesian track-localisation error for BRAN only.

**C. Untried or under-tried, as far as I found**
- Explicit beaching and refloating models: the "abrasion under barnacles" claim implies refloating.
- Item-specific leeway categories for interior panels vs. control surfaces.
- Several reanalyses run jointly rather than one model.
- Formal use of the WA non-detection.
- Joint isotope + find-time inversion. Herbert used isotopes only; drift studies used find times only.
- Using GDP **hourly** data to calibrate the near-surface inertial/Stokes response of undrogued drifters.
- Open-sourcing a common debris table so results can be compared.

**D. Compute**
- A 1–5 million particle ensemble over ~2 years is feasible on a workstation with Parcels/OpenDrift if fields are subset to 50°S–0°, 20–120°E.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the current download URLs, licences or exact product IDs (e.g., GLORYS12V1, the GDP hourly DOI, BRAN2020 THREDDS endpoint) in this session.
- Not checked: whether CSIRO's BRAN2015 drift-trial outputs or the GEOMAR particle sets are public.
- Not checked: whether Blamart & Bassinot's (2016) raw isotope data are public beyond what Al-Qattan et al. reproduce in their SI.

## 5. Overlap of drift constraints with the 7th arc, and what remains unsearched after the 2025–26 Ocean Infinity campaign

### Takeaway
Ocean Infinity's 2025–26 campaign covered ~7,571 km² of a 15,000 km² priority area over 28 operational days, without success. That area is centred on the IG/UGIB hotspot near 34.2°S 93.8°E and includes the CAPTION (Blelly/Marchand) area. 7,428.54 km² remains under a contract running to 30 June 2027. Operations are paused over the austral winter and early season, with resumption expected late 2026 or 2027. Sources conflict on the exact timing, and as of 5 Nov 2026 I found no report that the search had resumed.

The southern drift family (34–36°S) largely overlaps areas already searched in 2014–17, 2018 and 2025–26. Within that family, what remains are gaps, the outer glide width, and the remaining ~7,429 km². The northern drift family (~25–30°S) is the main drift-supported region that has never been searched to full width.

### Cited Findings
**2025 phase**
- Malaysia's Cabinet agreed the 15,000 km² area in principle on 13 Dec 2024, announced 20 Dec 2024. The search started ~25 Feb 2025 before the contract was signed (25/26 Mar 2025). It was suspended ~3 Apr 2025 for weather — [Wikipedia: MH370](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_Airlines_Flight_370); [AviationSourceNews](https://aviationsourcenews.com/mh370-search-is-the-new-ocean-infinity-search-about-to-begin/).
- Migflug instead describes the first phase as "four days in late March 2025" — [Migflug](https://migflug.com/afterburner/mh370-what-is-known-2026-ocean-infinity-search/) (conflict).
- In 2025 the vessel Armada 78-06 reportedly moved toward the Blelly/Marchand (CAPTION) recommended area — [mh370-caption.net](https://www.mh370-caption.net/index.php/2025-search-ocean-infinity/); [Radiant Physics, 31 Mar 2025](https://mh370.radiantphysics.com/2025/03/31/update-on-the-search-for-mh370/).

**2025–26 phase**
- Announced 3 Dec 2025 as a 55-day search. Armada 86 05 with two AUVs sailed from Fremantle on 31 Dec 2025. The phase ended 23 Jan 2026 without success — [Wikipedia: MH370](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_Airlines_Flight_370).

### Inferences
- **Inside the 33–39°S band, drift adds little discriminating power.** Every southern-family estimate has ±1–2° uncertainty, roughly 110–220 km along the arc, which is comparable to the area already searched. Drift work there can at most re-rank the remaining ~7,429 km² and the off-arc glide zones (Zones 2–3).
- **North of ~32–33°S, drift is the main independent evidence.** If the satellite (BTO/BFO) and fuel constraints are relaxed (outside this note's scope), a decisive drift and barnacle test between "north" (25–30°S) and "south" (34–36°S) is the most useful open-data contribution. The likely deciders are flaperon leeway, WA non-detection, and isotope SST.
- **The oldest barnacles remain the highest-value unexploited physical evidence.** If released, a properly calibrated δ¹⁸O series from first settlement could, in principle, separate those latitude bands on SST alone (inference; depends on growth-model error).

### Gaps
- Official coordinates or polygons for the 15,000 km² area and the 7,571 km² covered were not found. Malaysia and Ocean Infinity have not, in the sources I found, published the swath map.
- I could not confirm the planned resumption date beyond the conflicting reports above.
- The exact northern limit of the full-width 2018 OI coverage was not verified this session.
- Whether any 2025–26 search segments were chosen using debris drift directly (as opposed to UGIB/IG flight-path, WSPR or CAPTION analyses) is not stated in the sources found.
