# MH370: Acoustic, Hydroacoustic, Infrasound, Seismic and Eyewitness-Sound Evidence, and How Accessible the Data Is

Compiled 5 November 2026. Time reference: last Inmarsat handshake (7th arc) at 00:19:29 UTC, 8 March 2014 ([Kadri 2024, Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-60529-1)). Throughout, **IMPACT** marks attempts to detect the ocean impact and **FLIGHT** marks attempts to detect the aircraft in flight. Station naming: the CTBTO stations are called "HA01/HA08" in ATSB and Curtin documents, and "H01W/H08S/H08N" in Kadri and LANL. They are the same stations.

---

## 1. CTBTO hydroacoustic stations HA01 (Cape Leeuwin) and HA08 (Diego Garcia): Curtin CMST (Alec Duncan) 2014 analysis

### Takeaway
Curtin's CMST found a low-frequency underwater event (IMPACT candidate) at about 01:32–01:34 UTC on 8 March 2014, first on an IMOS recorder off Rottnest Island/Perth Canyon and then confirmed at HA01. The bearing was about 301° (north-west). Curtin itself said the event was incompatible with the Inmarsat range data. A September 2014 update using a Scott Reef recorder moved the source to about 2.11°N, 69.31°E near the Carlsberg Ridge, and concluded it was most likely geological. The ATSB later said hydroacoustic analysis "did not contribute any useful new information to the search."

### Cited Findings
- **4 June 2014 Curtin media release (IMPACT):** A low-frequency signal arrived just after 01:30 UTC on 8 March at an IMOS passive acoustic observatory about 40 km west of Rottnest Island. It was matched at the CTBTO hydrophone station south-west of Cape Leeuwin. The three Cape Leeuwin hydrophones gave a bearing from the north-west, and the difference in arrival times between the two sites gave a rough range. Curtin said the source could have been MH370 hitting the ocean "or a natural event such as a small earth tremor". It also said the event was not compatible with the satellite "handshake" data, which it called the most reliable source, and that there were "large uncertainties in the estimate". Other Curtin recorders were still deployed at the time and were due to be analysed once recovered. — [Curtin University media release, 4 Jun 2014](https://www.curtin.edu.au/news/media-release/curtin-researchers-search-acoustic-evidence-mh370/)
- Duncan put the probability that the noise came from MH370 at "maybe 10 per cent". The ATSB agreed the result was "not compatible" with the search team's analysis of the most likely entry area. — [SMH/ACM syndication via Manning River Times](https://www.manningrivertimes.com.au/story/2329058/curtin-university-researchers-find-possible-acoustic-trace-of-mh370/?cs=12); [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/hunt-for-malaysian-airliner-bolstered-by-new-clue1/)
- The underlying technical report is *Duncan, A.J., Gavrilov, A.N. & McCauley, R.D., "Analysis of Low Frequency Underwater Acoustic Signals Possibly Related to the Loss of Malaysian Airlines Flight MH370", CMST Report 2014-30, June 2014* (cited as ref. 12 in Kadri 2024). — [Kadri 2024 reference list](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-60529-1)
- **ATSB, "The Operational Search for MH370" (3 Oct 2017), Appendix H (Curtin June 2014 report):**
  - Acoustic event at about 01:34 UTC 8 March 2014; calculated peak-energy arrival 01:33:44 UTC ±4 s.
  - Recorders: the IMOS recorder near Perth Canyon ("RCS"), CTBTO HA01, CTBTO HA08, the IMOS Scott Reef recorder and other Curtin/IMOS recorders.
  - Bearing from HA01: 301.6° ±0.75°. First location estimate: 5.93°S, 77.22°E.
  - Conclusion: compatible with the timing of the last handshake but incompatible with the satellite-to-aircraft range, so likely geological or unrelated to MH370.
  - [ATSB Operational Search report PDF](https://www.atsb.gov.au/sites/default/files/media/5773565/operational-search-for-mh370_final_3oct2017.pdf)
- **ATSB 2017, Appendix I (Curtin update dated 4 Sept 2014):**
  - The Scott Reef recorder showed a low-frequency arrival starting at 01:32:49 UTC. The revised source location was 2.11°N, 69.31°E, near the Carlsberg Ridge (north-west Indian Ocean, west of the Maldives).
  - Conclusion: "likely of geological origin".
  - The same appendix cites a University of Washington solution (Dall'Osto & Dahl) based on an HA08S arrival plus HA01 direction and timing data. That solution sits about 400 km north-west of the Carlsberg Ridge fix.
  - [ATSB 2017 PDF, App. I pp. ~418–426](https://www.atsb.gov.au/sites/default/files/media/5773565/operational-search-for-mh370_final_3oct2017.pdf)
- **ATSB 2017 main report (Hydroacoustic analysis section, about pp. 114–116):** At the "first principles review" the complete hydroacoustic results were presented, and participants decided that hydroacoustic analysis "did not contribute any useful new information to the search". The report also notes that whether an impact couples into the SOFAR channel depends on favourable bathymetry and water depth. — [ATSB 2017 PDF](https://www.atsb.gov.au/sites/default/files/media/5773565/operational-search-for-mh370_final_3oct2017.pdf)
- **Los Alamos National Laboratory (ATSB Appendix J; R.J. Stead, "Seismic and hydroacoustic analysis relevant to MH370", 3 July 2014) (IMPACT):**
  - Only three useful stations were found: H01, H08 and the Geoscope seismic station AIS (Amsterdam Island).
  - H01 had "an interesting arrival" at about 00:52 UTC. It was compromised by an overlapping dispersive Antarctic ice-event signal (azimuth 190.5°, phase velocity 1.46 km/s).
  - H08 was obscured by large-amplitude repeating signals. AIS was too noisy at low frequency and had a low sample rate.
  - The results were inconclusive but "pointed to a more southerly impact location" within the search region.
  - The report is publicly available on OSTI.
  - Sources: [ATSB 2017 PDF](https://www.atsb.gov.au/sites/default/files/media/5773565/operational-search-for-mh370_final_3oct2017.pdf); [OSTI 1136454](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1136454)
- Sources disagree on bearing and timing details:
  - Kadri 2024 cites the Curtin event at H01W as 01:34:40 UTC, bearing 301.4° ±0.4°.
  - ATSB Appendix H gives 301.6° ±0.75°.
  - Independent analyst Ed Anderson (370Location.org) gives an original ~301.6° refined to ~300.93–300.95° after a 2019 hydrophone-position recalibration, with H01 arrival about 01:34:50 UTC.
  - Anderson states the event was not seen at H08 because the topography blocks paths from the north-west. His page also misprints Curtin's fix as "69.31S"; the ATSB has 69.31°E.
  - Sources: [Kadri 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-60529-1); [370Location.org, 15 Sep 2017 (updated 2019)](https://370location.org/2017/09/mh370-locating-the-curtin-event-origin-with-seismometers)

### Inferences
- There were two distinct HA01 "events":
  - the **01:3x UTC "Curtin event"**, which is too late for an impact near the 7th arc and was relocated to the north-west Indian Ocean (geological);
  - the **~00:52–00:54 UTC LANL/Kadri candidate** on bearing ~306°, which fits the timing better but was contaminated by an Antarctic ice signal and seen at only one station.

  Writers should not conflate the two.
- No official body has ever claimed a confirmed acoustic detection of the MH370 impact.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the CMST 2014-30 report PDF directly. Its content is taken from the ATSB appendices as summarised by a PDF-parsing tool, so page numbers are approximate.
- The University of Washington (Dall'Osto & Dahl 2014) HA08S report appears never to have been formally published; it is cited only in ATSB Appendix I and Kadri 2024.
- A blog claim that the HA08 data had a 25-minute outage could not be verified against a CTBTO statement (see §2).

## 2. Usama Kadri (Cardiff University): acoustic-gravity wave work on MH370

### Takeaway
Kadri's line of work runs from 2017 to 2019 (with Crivelli and others) and continues in a 2024 single-author *Scientific Reports* paper and a September 2025 CTBTO SnT poster. It argues that ocean-impacting aircraft leave hydroacoustic and acoustic-gravity-wave signatures detectable at 2,000–5,000 km. For MH370 it finds only **one** candidate signal: H01W, ~00:52–00:54 UTC, bearing ~306°, not seen at Diego Garcia. Kadri proposes controlled explosions or airgun shots along the 7th arc to calibrate. As of November 2026 I found no evidence these experiments were carried out.

### Cited Findings
- **2017:** Kadri, Crivelli, Parsons, Colbourne & Ryan, "Rewinding the waves: tracking underwater signals to their source", *Sci Rep* 7:13949. They tested the method with 18 weighted spheres dropped into water tanks, then applied it to Indian Ocean hydrophone data from around the time of the disappearance. — [Kadri 2024 ref. list](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-60529-1); [Cardiff ORCA](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/119016); [Live Science coverage](https://www.livescience.com/64861-lost-malaysia-mh370-crash-site-sounds.html)
- **2019:** Kadri, "Effect of sea-bottom elasticity on the propagation of acoustic–gravity waves from impacting objects", *Sci Rep* 9:919. Accounting for seafloor elasticity moved the candidate source points farther from the stations. Two possible locations were identified. Co-author Crivelli cautioned that they could not say "with any real certainty" that these were linked to the aircraft. — [PMC6351681](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6351681/); [Newstalk ZB summary](https://www.newstalkzb.co.nz/news/world/mathematician-unveils-new-theory-on-where-mh370-went-down/)
- Per Kadri 2024, the 2017/2019 studies placed the Curtin 01:34:40 UTC H01W event about 1,900 ±200 km from H01W, centred near 23.662°S, 96.676°E, with source time between 01:11 and 01:16 UTC. — [Kadri 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-60529-1)
- **2024:** Kadri, "Underwater acoustic analysis reveals unique pressure signals associated with aircraft crashes in the sea: revisiting MH370", *Sci Rep* 14:10102, published 2 May 2024 (open access). It covers both IMPACT and FLIGHT.
  - Ten historical aircraft crashes plus one submarine loss were analysed (>100 h of data). Distinct pressure signals were detected at 2,211–4,695 km.
  - **For MH370 (IMPACT):** He examined HA01 (H01W) and HA08 (H08S/N) within minutes of the 7th-arc time. "If MH370 crashed in the vicinity of the 7th arc, hydroacoustic signals should have been detected at least at H01W." Only one signal of interest was found: H01W, 00:52 UTC (Table 1: 00:54 UTC), bearing 306° (306.18°).
  - Confirmation at H08S was hampered by very high noise ("most likely airgun") and a large bathymetric rise along the path. H04 (Crozet) was not operational in 2014 and stayed down until 2017.
  - **FLIGHT/early path:** He checked the initial-route stage near the last ATC contact (Gulf of Thailand). "No signals of interest were found."
  - **Recommendation:** controlled explosions along the 7th arc with energy similar to MH370's impact, on the model of the ARA San Juan calibration grenade.
  - Source: [Nature Sci Rep 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-60529-1)
- Kadri's Conversation article (10 Jun 2024) repeats the findings:
  - One relevant signal toward the 7th arc at Cape Leeuwin, not detected at Diego Garcia, "which raises questions about its origin".
  - No signatures along the initial flight path. The findings are "not conclusive" because few past incidents are available for comparison.
  - It proposes explosions or airguns as calibration: if the test signals match the candidate's amplitude, focus the search there; if they are much stronger at both stations, the candidate needs re-examination.
  - [The Conversation, 10 Jun 2024](https://theconversation.com/flight-mh370-can-underwater-sound-signals-solve-aviations-greatest-mystery-230264)
- Kadri on the value of a null result: "If we run the experiments and we record nothing at the two stations, we cannot rely on the available hydrophone data to assist for the search." — [Changing Times, 22 Jun 2024](https://changingtimes.media/2024/06/22/mh370-experimental-ocean-blasts-could-bring-investigators-closer-to-the-truth/); [Tech Times, 21 May 2024](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/304834/20240521/finding-long-lost-mh370-possible-underwater-signals-open-sea-airplane-crashes-study.htm)
- **2025:** Kadri presented e-poster P1.4-254, "Unveiling Hydroacoustic Signatures of Aircraft Crashes: Insights from the MH370 Case", at CTBTO SnT2025 (Vienna, 11 Sep 2025).
  - It reports "plausible signals" within MH370's expected time and space window, and notes bathymetric barriers and noise interference.
  - It again proposes controlled-explosion validation. The poster is single-author and builds on the 2024 paper.
  - [CTBTO SnT2025 contribution 5606](https://conferences.ctbto.org/event/30/contributions/5606/); [poster PDF](https://conferences.ctbto.org/event/30/contributions/5606/attachments/3236/6379/P1.4-254_Kadri.pdf)
- Kadri has said publicly that an unexplained ~25-minute shutdown of HA08S around the relevant period has not been explained by the CTBTO. — [Newstalk ZB](https://www.newstalkzb.co.nz/news/world/mathematician-unveils-new-theory-on-where-mh370-went-down/). The 2024 paper itself, as extracted, attributes the HA08S problems to airgun noise, not to a data gap. This is unresolved.
- Independent, non-peer-reviewed acoustic work also exists:
  - Ed Anderson / 370Location.org, e.g. a Feb 2018 post on a "strong anomalous acoustic event on the seventh arc near Java".
  - ResearchGate preprints such as "Sounding-Out MH370 Locations" and "Discovery of Surface Sounds from MH370's Ditching at the Penang-Longitude Hole".
  - None of these have been endorsed by the ATSB or Malaysia.
  - Sources: [370Location 2018](https://370location.org/2018/02/a-strong-anomalous-acoustic-event-on-the-seventh-arc-near-java/); [ResearchGate 383240262](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/383240262_Sounding-Out_MH370_Locations); [ResearchGate 398440271](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398440271_Discovery_of_Surface_Sounds_from_MH370's_Ditching_at_the_Penang-Longitude_Hole)

### Inferences
- The ~00:52–00:54 UTC H01W arrival on bearing ~306° appears to be the same arrival LANL flagged in July 2014 (Appendix J). Kadri's 2024 work independently re-surfaces the official 2014 candidate rather than producing a new detection.
- The time from 00:19 UTC to an H01W arrival at ~00:52–00:54 UTC implies roughly 30+ minutes of propagation at ~1.48 km/s (about 2,700–3,000 km). That is broadly consistent with a source on the mid-to-northern 7th arc NW of Cape Leeuwin. This is my own arithmetic, not a published localisation.
- Without a second-station fix the candidate gives a bearing but no range. That is why calibration shots are central to the proposal.

### Gaps
- I found no report (to Nov 2026) that the ATSB, Malaysia, Ocean Infinity or the CTBTO conducted Kadri's proposed controlled-explosion or airgun calibration along the 7th arc.
- I found no peer-reviewed 2025–2026 Kadri journal paper beyond the SnT2025 poster.
- I found no CTBTO statement explaining or confirming the alleged ~25-minute HA08 gap.

## 3. Infrasound and seismometers (IMS and national networks)

### Takeaway
The CTBTO's International Data Centre (IDC) ran an infrasound review in the first days (FLIGHT and early crash hypothesis near Malaysia/Vietnam). It found **no detection** within 5,000 km and stated that network capability in the region was poor. Seismic data was used only marginally:
- LANL: AIS on Amsterdam Island, which was too noisy.
- Independent analysts: public IRIS/GEOFON stations in the Maldives and India, for the Curtin event.
- A Chinese (USTC) claim of a "seafloor event" off Vietnam on the early-path hypothesis, which was overtaken by the later radar/satellite reconstruction.

No infrasound analysis of the southern Indian Ocean end-of-flight period was found.

### Cited Findings
- **CTBTO, 14 Mar 2014:**
  - Member States requested technical assistance and data.
  - Executive Secretary Lassina Zerbo: "So far the processing of our data has not provided information as to the whereabouts of the plane", and he encouraged Member State scientists to study the data.
  - CTBTO said aircraft accidents may be detected by infrasound (explosions), seismic (impact of a larger aircraft on the ground) and hydroacoustic stations (impact of a large aircraft into the oceans).
  - Past examples: the FedEx crash at Narita (2009) and two F-16s in Belgium (2003) were detected only by infrasound stations "within a few tens to a few hundreds of kilometres".
  - [CTBTO news, 14 Mar 2014](https://www.ctbto.org/news-and-events/news/researchers-encouraged-probe-ctbto-monitoring-data-signs-missing-malaysian)
- **CTBTO IDC brief "IDC infrasound search for missing flight Malaysian Airlines MH370" (FLIGHT and early crash; undated, about 10–14 Mar 2014):**
  - Commercial aircraft in normal flight are usually detected only within about 100 km of a station.
  - The closest certified station to Kuala Lumpur was IS06 (Cocos Island), about 1,600–1,700 km away. Next were IS52 (Diego Garcia) and IS39 (Palau) at about 3,500 km, then IS04/IS07 (Australia) beyond 4,300 km.
  - Equatorial stratospheric winds made long-range propagation unfavourable, and network capability was "fair to weak/poor".
  - "IDC bulletins and interactive review analysis results for all stations located within 5000km from the search area did not provide any detection or trace of signals related to the missing flight MH370."
  - IS30 (Japan) had detections from that azimuth, which were attributed to volcanoes and microbaroms.
  - [CTBTO IDC PDF](https://www.ctbto.org/sites/default/files/Documents/IDC_infrasound_MalaysianAirlines_MH370.pdf)
- Contemporary coverage: the CTBTO said neither an explosion nor a crash had been detected. — [Jalopnik](https://jalopnik.com/u-n-nuke-watchdog-no-mh370-explosion-or-plane-crash-1546534693); [CBS News](https://cbsnews.com/news/missing-malaysia-air-flight-370-nuclear-test-experts-to-see-if-missing-plane-exploded)
- **Seismic, LANL 2014 (IMPACT):** The only seismic station found useful was Geoscope AIS (Amsterdam Island). It was obscured by low-frequency noise and limited by a low sample rate. — [ATSB 2017 App. J](https://www.atsb.gov.au/sites/default/files/media/5773565/operational-search-for-mh370_final_3oct2017.pdf); [OSTI 1136454](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1136454)
- **Seismic, independent (Ed Anderson, 370Location, 2017–2019):** He used public seismometers to look for T-phase arrivals.
  - IN.MNC (Minicoy, India): event at 00:28:40 UTC.
  - GE.KAAM (Kaadedhdhoo, Maldives): event at 00:30:00 UTC.
  - He proposed a Carlsberg Ridge candidate near 5.43°N, 66.16°E and called it "not a prime candidate location".
  - Unsuitable stations: II.DGAR (Diego Garcia) is blocked by the Chagos Bank, II.MSEY (Seychelles) has shallow paths, GE.SOCY showed no matching arrivals, and GE.HMDM was offline.
  - The data came from the public IRIS/FDSN/GFZ archives.
  - [370Location.org, Sep 2017](https://370location.org/2017/09/mh370-locating-the-curtin-event-origin-with-seismometers)
- **Seismic, USTC (early-path hypothesis):** The University of Science and Technology of China reported a small "seafloor event" at 2:55 a.m. on 8 March (local time; ~1.5 h after loss of contact), about 116 km north-east of the last tracked position, south of Vietnam. It was detected by two stations in Malaysia. The university said that because it was a non-seismic zone it "might be related" to MH370. — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/storyline/missing-jet/seismic-event-close-missing-jet-path-china-scientists-n52516); [The Star, 15 Mar 2014](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/regional/2014/03/15/seafloor-event-possibly-linked-to-mh370-say-researchers)

### Inferences
- The USTC event location is inconsistent with the later accepted path (turn-back across Malaysia, then south over the Indian Ocean) and was effectively superseded. I found no formal rebuttal.
- An impact in the southern Indian Ocean at ~00:19 UTC sits several thousand km from any IMS infrasound station. Given the CTBTO's own ~100 km detection rule-of-thumb for aircraft in normal flight, infrasound detection of either the flight or the impact was implausible. Hydroacoustics was the only realistic geophysical channel.
- Global seismic and IRIS/GEOFON data are freely downloadable, so seismic T-phase re-analysis is reproducible by anyone. IMS seismic, infrasound and hydroacoustic data are not freely available (see §6).

### Gaps
- I found no published infrasound analysis targeted at the 7th-arc end-of-flight time window (stations IS04 Shannon WA, IS06 Cocos, IS52 Diego Garcia). The IDC brief covered only the initial South China Sea search area.
- I found no Indonesian (BMKG) or Australian (Geoscience Australia) seismic-network assessment specific to MH370.
- I found no formal official assessment of the USTC seafloor-event claim.

## 4. IMOS passive acoustic observatory moorings (Perth Canyon, Portland, etc.): use for MH370 and public accessibility

### Takeaway
IMOS data was central to the 2014 work. Curtin's initial detection came from the IMOS Perth Canyon recorder west of Rottnest Island, and the IMOS Scott Reef recorder was used for the September 2014 relocation. Perth Canyon (PAPCA) data covers 2008–2017, so it includes March 2014, and it is openly licensed (CC BY 4.0) via the AODN. However, the recorders were duty-cycled (3–8 min every 15 min at 6 kHz), so coverage of any given minute is not guaranteed.

### Cited Findings
- Curtin's lead came from "a passive acoustic observatory about 40 km west of Rottnest Island" that is part of IMOS. — [Curtin, 4 Jun 2014](https://www.curtin.edu.au/news/media-release/curtin-researchers-search-acoustic-evidence-mh370/)
- The ATSB appendices list the IMOS recorder near the Perth Canyon ("RCS") and the IMOS recorder off Scott Reef (NW Australia) among the instruments used. The Scott Reef record supplied the 01:32:49 UTC arrival used to relocate the event to the Carlsberg Ridge. — [ATSB 2017 Apps H & I](https://www.atsb.gov.au/sites/default/files/media/5773565/operational-search-for-mh370_final_3oct2017.pdf)
- **IMOS ANMN Passive Acoustic Observatory, Perth Canyon (PAPCA):**
  - Temporal coverage: 26 Feb 2008 to 26 Aug 2017. Decommissioned October 2017.
  - Hydrophones on the seabed at 400–490 m depth, in a four-mooring triangle with ~5 km sides plus a centre mooring, at about 31.89°S, 114.94°E.
  - Recording: 6 kHz for 3–8 minutes every 15 minutes, calibrated down to ~1–2 Hz.
  - Access is "Open". Raw data is available "on portable disk from the AODN" under a CC BY 4.0 licence.
  - [Research Data Australia PAPCA record](https://researchdata.edu.au/imos-anmn-passive-wa-papca/476472); [AODN metadata](https://catalogue-imos.aodn.org.au:443/geonetwork/srv/api/records/9f7a31dc-d7d8-4718-bec8-618829bcb8d3)
- **Other IMOS observatories:** Portland, Vic (PAPOR, ~170 m depth, data 2009 to 12 Feb 2018), Tuncurry/Forster and Kangaroo Island, all decommissioned 2017/18.
  - Raw files are binary with text headers. CMST provides Matlab tools, and an AODN acoustic data viewer is available for previewing and downloading.
  - [data.gov.au IMOS PAO sub-facility](https://data.gov.au/data/dataset/4f0b036a-d7a4-4f78-821e-b9461ccb5b6d); [Research Data Australia](https://researchdata.edu.au/imos-anmn-passive-sub-facility/476795)

### Inferences
- Of all the hydroacoustic data relevant to MH370, the Perth Canyon IMOS record for 8 March 2014 is the most openly accessible piece (open licence, no contract). That makes it the realistic entry point for independent re-analysis.
- It is a shallow (~400–490 m) shelf-edge site and its duty cycle could miss a short transient. Its geometry also differs from HA01's SOFAR-axis hydrophones.
- The Portland, Tuncurry and Kangaroo Island sites are on the south and east coasts and are probably poorly placed for 7th-arc paths. Their data may still exist for March 2014.

### Gaps
- I did not verify in the AODN viewer that files for 8 March 2014 exist for PAPCA or for the Scott Reef recorder. The Scott Reef recorder appears to have been a Curtin/IMOS deployment; I found no public dataset listing for it.
- The exact duty-cycle schedule in March 2014, and whether the 00:19–01:40 UTC window was captured, was not confirmed.

## 5. Eyewitness sound and sight reports (FLIGHT)

### Takeaway
All the prominent eyewitness reports date from 2014 and were investigated, at most, by local police or handed to the DCA, JACC or ATSB. None were corroborated or accepted by investigators:
- Kuda Huvadhoo (Maldives) low-flying jet and noise.
- Kate Tee's "burning plane" in the Andaman Sea/Indian Ocean.
- Mike McKay's oil-rig sighting off Vietnam.
- Kelantan/Terengganu fishermen and villagers (low-flying lights, jet-engine noise).

The Maldives report also conflicts with the timing implied by the Inmarsat data.

### Cited Findings
- **Kuda Huvadhoo, Maldives (FLIGHT):**
  - Residents told the outlet Haveeru that a large white aircraft with red stripes flew very low at around 06:15 local time (01:15 UTC) on 8 March, heading south-east toward the atoll's southern tip. One witness said they could see the doors. The reports were consistent about the low-flying, south-easterly track between 06:15 and 08:00.
  - Maldives police opened an inquiry. The MNDF said military radar had picked up no trace of the flight.
  - Malaysia's Defence Minister said Maldivian authorities told him the sightings were not true.
  - Sources: [Inquirer/AFP](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/587161/maldives-probe-reports-of-possible-mh370-sighting); [Minivan News archive](https://minivannewsarchive.com/tag/mh370); [Mediaite](https://www.mediaite.com/online/maldives-island-residents-claim-to-have-seen-low-flying-jet-on-night-of-mh370s-disappearance)
- In April 2015 witnesses (first reported by *The Weekend Australian*) described a very large low plane with red and blue markings, and some only heard "a loud noise" after it passed. They complained investigators ignored them. An unnamed source close to the police inquiry said they had "no motive to lie". This account describes the event as a Saturday evening, which conflicts with the 06:15 morning time in the 2014 reports. — [Malay Mail, 5 Apr 2015](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2015/04/05/in-maldives-frustration-over-ignored-claims-of-mh370-sightings/872957)
- **Kate Tee (FLIGHT):**
  - A British sailor on night watch aboard a yacht sailing from Kochi to Phuket on 8 March 2014. She reported seeing what looked like a burning aircraft and later checked her GPS log.
  - Volunteer analysts (e.g. Stewart Stoddart) found her track consistent with the reconstructed flight path.
  - She reported to the JACC, which said the information was "forwarded to the ATSB's MH370 Search Strategy Working Group for review". It is unclear whether there was any further assessment.
  - Sources: [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/oceania/mh370-search-did-sailor-see-plane-on-fire-1.1343029); [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/581691); [IBTimes AU](https://ibtimes.com.au/mh370-woman-sailor-finally-speaks-seeing-plane-fire-files-report-jacc-1342778); [The Thaiger](https://thethaiger.com/news/phuket/New-crowdsourced-data-shows-Phuket-yacht-sighting-MH370-flight-path-correlate)
- **Mike McKay (FLIGHT):**
  - A New Zealand worker on the Songa Mercur rig off Vietnam. He emailed his employer about seeing a burning object. The email leaked and he later lost his job.
  - His sighting lies over the South China Sea, which is inconsistent with the later radar/satellite reconstruction (turn-back west, then south).
  - [IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/missing-flight-mh370-oil-rig-worker-sacked-after-reporting-sighting-mh370-fire-1451804)
- **Kelantan/Terengganu (FLIGHT, sound):**
  - Four police reports from Kota Bharu (Kuala Besar, Pantai Cahaya Bulan), Tumpat (Geting) and Bachok (Pantai Senok) described a low-flying aircraft at 01:30–01:45 local time heading north toward the South China Sea.
  - A bus driver reported a low plane with blinking beacons at Penarik, Setiu, at about 03:45.
  - Eight villagers at Kampung Pantai Seberang, Marang, reported a loud noise at about 01:20 local "like a jet engine fan" from north-east of Pulau Kapas, but saw nothing.
  - Kelantan police handed the four reports to the Kelantan DCA. No further assessment was reported.
  - Sources: [The Star, 12 Mar 2014](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2014/03/12/cops-get-reports-on-lowflying-aircraft-and-loud-noise); [Malay Mail, 12 Mar 2014](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2014/03/12/kelantan-police-hand-over-witness-reports-to-dca/633373); [The Star, 11 Mar 2014](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2014/03/11/kelantan-duo-report-seeing-lights-falling-at-high-speed)

### Inferences
- The Kelantan/Marang reports (01:20–01:45 local = 17:20–17:45 UTC on 7 March) are roughly contemporaneous with the transponder loss (~17:21 UTC) and the turn-back over the Malay Peninsula. They are the most temporally plausible "sound" reports. However, the witnesses describe a northbound heading, while the radar-reconstructed path is westbound.
- A Kuda Huvadhoo sighting at ~01:15 UTC is about an hour after the 00:19 UTC final handshake. It is geometrically incompatible with the 7th arc (far to the south-east). Accepting it would require rejecting the Inmarsat analysis, which the ATSB did not do.

### Gaps
- I found no official ATSB or Malaysian Safety Investigation Team document that explicitly evaluates and rejects each eyewitness report. I did not search the 2018 Malaysian Safety Investigation Report for these.
- I found no original Haveeru article (that outlet is now defunct); the accounts come through AFP and Minivan reproductions.

## 6. CTBTO data access rules (vDEC, civil applications) and whether MH370-period data was released

### Takeaway
CTBTO IMS data for March 2014 was shared with Member States' National Data Centres and, on request, with researchers through the vDEC. It is **not publicly downloadable**. vDEC access is free but contract-bound: organisational applicants, named authorised persons, no redistribution and no publication of raw waveforms. Kadri's 2024 paper states that the MH370 hydroacoustic data "cannot be shared by the author" and must be requested from the CTBTO.

### Cited Findings
- On 14 Mar 2014 the CTBTO publicly encouraged Member-State scientists to study its data.
  - Access for Member States' experts is via the CTBTO Web Portal.
  - "Access to IMS data for scientific and research institutions" is via the vDEC, by contacting support@ctbto.org.
  - [CTBTO, 14 Mar 2014](https://www.ctbto.org/news-and-events/news/researchers-encouraged-probe-ctbto-monitoring-data-signs-missing-malaysian)
- vDEC terms:
  - Access is free and generally open to organisations rather than individuals, which must sign a contract.
  - Projects are reviewed for scientific viability and policy alignment.
  - Up to three named authorised persons (nationalities declared). Users receive only the data their study needs, supplied "as is".
  - No redistribution to third parties. Raw data may not be published, though results may be. CTBTO acknowledgement is requested.
  - [CTBTO vDEC page](https://www.ctbto.org/specials/vdec/); [CTBTO For Researchers](https://www.ctbto.org/resources/for-researchers-experts); [AGU 2018 abstract on vDEC](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018AGUFM.S53D0430M/abstract)
- Kadri 2024 data availability statement: the hydroacoustic data "were obtained from the CTBTO and cannot be shared by the author to third parties, though can be requested directly from CTBTO". IMS hydroacoustic data "is available to National Data Centres of the CTBTO and can be made available to others on request through the virtual Data Exploitation Center (vDEC)". — [Kadri 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-60529-1)
- Independent analyst Ed Anderson notes CTBTO hydrophone data is restricted to qualified researchers. He relied on public seismic data instead. — [370Location.org](https://370location.org/2017/09/mh370-locating-the-curtin-event-origin-with-seismometers)

### Inferences
- **The MH370-period IMS data was "released" only in a restricted sense:**
  - to Australian authorities (Curtin worked through the ATSB/Australian NDC arrangements);
  - to LANL (the US NDC);
  - to the University of Washington;
  - to Kadri via vDEC.

  It was never put into the public domain.
- Anyone wanting to reproduce the H01W 00:52 UTC or 01:34 UTC analyses needs an institutional vDEC contract. The open alternatives are:
  - IMOS/AODN Perth Canyon data (CC BY 4.0);
  - IRIS/GEOFON seismic data;
  - the LANL report (OSTI);
  - the ATSB appendices, which contain figures but not raw waveforms.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the current (2026) vDEC contract version or the exact data-retention period for 2014 IMS hydroacoustic data. The CTBTO pages returned 403 to direct fetch; the terms above come from search-indexed CTBTO content and may be dated.
- I found no evidence of any special CTBTO "civil application" public release of the MH370 window.
